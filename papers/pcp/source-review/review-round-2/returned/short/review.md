# Specialist PCP account: substantive review

## Judgment and audience

The strongest result recovered is the stated PCP characterization with one fixed polynomial-time implementation per NP language, exactly an affine ceiling-logarithmic fair-bit budget, a constant upper bound on proof-bit queries, polynomially bounded readable addresses, perfect completeness and soundness at most one half. The proof is chosen before the seed. The converse uses a finite proof prefix and exhaustive enumeration of the polynomially many seeds. The specialist text preserves these requirements; the checked formal declarations and definition bridges support them. I found no false central theorem, invalid central algebraic implication or incorrect displayed numerical bound in the reviewed scope. This is a source-based mathematical and expository audit, not a new Lean build or kernel replay.

That positive fidelity judgment is compatible with a substantial revision recommendation. The paper explains several individual devices well, but sometimes compresses the operation of a test into a list of mathematical ingredients. The reader then has to recover which table is queried, which random quantities are shared, which distribution is uniform, and whether acceptance is conjunctive. Those are proof-relevant facts, especially in the correction and common-decoding arguments. The most useful next version would expose these facts at their first use, supply two missing short deductions, and spend less main-route space on large decimal constants. It does not need a different PCP construction or a generic expansion of background.

The three requested perspectives lead to distinct assessments. For a **source owner**, the account has value beyond a theorem-and-definitions guide because it connects bit observations, decoding and vanishing certificates into a soundness proof. Its exact distinctions between Lee's implementation and v2 should be retained. Blanket credit for both encodings, the machine-model wording, and an indirect route to the derived ZERO theorem create avoidable correction work. For a **PCP researcher familiar with v2**, the formal implementation differences and the rejection-dependent correction argument justify reading the account; routine algebra and large numerical bounds have lower marginal value. For a **complexity theorist who knows PCP but not this construction**, the first substantial obstacle is §4's claim of simultaneous full-label and homogeneous-label decoding without the restricted-label deduction. The next is the terse operational description of the outer test. Such a reader can reconstruct the route with the long companion, but the present specialist text asks them to leave it at precisely the steps it advertises as its contribution. These are analytical judgments, not observed reactions from human readers or any named author.

The full sequential first reading was preserved before consulting the companion, proofs and prior audits. It was primed by reading assignment.md too early, and the opening slice extended into §3; first_reading.md states both deviations. The initial account correctly recovered the theorem, the two encodings and the common-decoding goal. It did not independently reconstruct the bivariate theorem or the missing pointwise estimate in weighted robustness. Later source checks resolved the protocol and numerical questions; they did not retroactively improve that first account.

## What the construction needs each layer to do

The proof has a coherent spine. Three colours are represented by the nonzero elements of the embedded four-element field. A polynomial P extends the colouring and E extends adjacency. The vertex residual P³−1 enforces colour membership, while E((P(X)−P(Y))³−1) enforces unequal adjacent colours. The K₄ example in §2 is unusually effective: assigning all four elements of F₄ passes the edge equations, while the zero-coloured vertex fails the membership equation. It shows why both constraints are necessary rather than merely naming them. Keep it.

Grid vanishing is encoded by two identities for a certificate M: M(X,0)=0 and M(X,Z_H(X))=A(X). The repeated monic-division argument is enough for this audience, and the converse for a certificate nonlinear in its second block is a strong explanatory sentence: on the grid the two arguments coincide. Add only the honest degree calculation. A nonzero quotient U_i has degree at most deg A−h, so Y_iU_i has degree at most deg A because h≥1; zero quotients cause no problem. This is a small omission, not a failed bound. The public E belongs to the verifier computation, not the prover's tables. [Short §§2–3, lines 44–108; v2 Lemma 2.9 and the inspected grid-certificate source route.]

The first encoding turns a line polynomial of degree below b^c into a polynomial of total degree at most c on cb variables. The substitution Φ recovers its value. The crucial asymmetry is already stated well: an arbitrary decoded polynomial U need not have the honest set-multilinear form, and U∘Φ has the larger bound D=cb^c. The field and root estimates must use D. Do not simplify this into an assertion that the received line row is itself an honest encoding.

The second encoding represents a field value by its evaluations under low-degree Boolean labels. A table address consists of a point and a label; the stored object is a bit. Cubic labels implement the nonlinear constraints. Homogeneous linear labels implement weighted field sums. Fresh uniform full-label masks implement a requested special label with two reads. Each marginal read is uniform even when the requested label is highly biased. Their independence is unnecessary: the union bound gives error at most twice the full-label distance. The short paper's explanation of this masking step is already sufficient. [Short lines 112–144, equations (6)–(9).]

The opening correctly explains why a field symbol is not one bit query, but omits a second resource obstacle. Directly applying the binary encoding to the entire growing coefficient vector can produce a quasipolynomial proof and superlogarithmic randomness. The digit encoding first reduces the degree to a fixed constant; the low-degree Hadamard layer is then applied to field values. The supplied v2 introduction explicitly motivates this order and distinguishes the established Hadamard construction from the new set-multilinear inverse-Kronecker variant. R01 supplies this motivation. This addition makes the design necessary rather than merely plausible. [v2 tex/intro.tex, lines 24–54; short line 30.]

## The hard steps: what reconstructs and what should be added

### Weighted robustness needs its pointwise hinge

The exact weighted test uses c+1 distinct nonzero powers of a generator of the **full** multiplicative group, with nonzero Lagrange weights. Those hypotheses are material. Generator propagation produces low-degree restrictions on every line; the finite-field line characterization then produces total degree at most c. In characteristic two, q>2c implies the sufficient condition q/2−1≥c in Friedl–Sudan's corrected Lemma 4. The historical order-three/unit-weight example does not invalidate the corrected test.

For the robust statement, let n=c+1 and let A_f(x,v) be the weighted prediction of f(x). Define g(x) by plurality over v. The short text explains Δ(f,g)≤2δ, then jumps from a symmetric weighted grid to exact satisfaction. The missing displayed hinge is

\[
\forall x,\qquad \Pr_v[A_f(x,v)\ne g(x)]\le 2n\delta.
\]

This is a uniform statement about a fixed target, obtained by comparing two predictions through the double grid. Together with the original test it gives three losses 2nδ, nδ and 2n²δ. Their sum is below one under the printed δ<1/[2(c+2)²], because 2(c+2)²−(2(c+1)²+3(c+1))=c+3>0. Positive probability then forces the two fixed compared values to agree. The supplied long proof and WeightedConstraintSoundness.lean contain this argument. The short paragraph is a correct sketch, but displaying this estimate would let a specialist recover its budget. If it is left out, explicitly label the paragraph a sketch and name this exact delegated step. [Short lines 167–174; long lines 228–295; weighted source, prediction_error_le and correction_satisfies.]

### One decoded message must support both distances

The Hadamard-row paragraph explains how additivity, unit preservation and multiplicativity force an evaluation functional. Its bilinear-defect argument is useful: a nonzero defect occupies at least one quarter of its domain, while replacing three corrected values and charging the test costs at most 7ε. Below 1/28 the defect therefore vanishes. This establishes evaluation structure for one field value.

It does not, by itself, explain the subsequent restricted-distance claim. The affine consistency check fails with probability at most ε. Replacing its masked answer by the same linear functional costs at most 2ε. Thus the received affine labels differ from that same evaluation word on at most 3ε of affine positions. Homogeneous labels form half the affine domain, giving a bound of 6ε there. Full-label error is at most ε. At rejection at least 1/28, an arbitrary value and the trivial distance bound one give the common bound 28ε on both domains. This is the missing deduction, and it is already in the source mathematics. [Short lines 187–193; HadamardSoundness.lean and HadamardRowDecoding.lean; long Hadamard-testing section.]

I tried the tempting simplification of keeping only full-label proximity. It fails. For t=4 and r=3, the label space has dimension 1+4+6+4=15 and hence 32,768 labels. Only 16 labels are homogeneous linear. Starting with an exact evaluation word, flip every homogeneous-label bit. The full distance is 16/32,768=1/2048; the homogeneous distance is one. This is a counterexample to the proposed inference from full distance to restricted distance, **not** a cheating proof for the combined Hadamard test. It explains why the affine consistency test and the same-message conclusion earn their space. The exact counts were independently recomputed and are recorded in evidence/arithmetic_checks.json.

The same discipline must persist through inner decoding. The two tester branches each reject with probability at most 2κ, giving row errors at most 56κ. Weighted relabelling preserves uniform homogeneous labels. The c+2 replacements and the binary-to-field factor two give field rejection at most [112(c+2)+4]κ. Applying the scalar theorem in its valid regime and the trivial bound outside it yields the stated unconditional Kκ bounds for one polynomial. “Decode the rows” is a choice made in soundness analysis; it is not an additional globally executed verifier algorithm. [Short lines 197–204; BinaryLocalRowDecoding.lean.]

### The outer test should be an experiment before it is an estimate

Specify one run. Sample u,v,z uniformly, including zero values. Test the primary Hadamard row at u. Test the auxiliary row for that same oriented line (u,v) using the inner tester. Use a random homogeneous label to compare the primary bit at u+zv with the corrected auxiliary value at Φ(z). Use the prescribed independent tester seeds and accept only when all three checks pass. That description makes the subsequent bound auditable: the corrected auxiliary contribution is (c+1)Kκ, the observed comparison contributes κ, and the primary replacement contributes 28κ. Equation (7) doubles their sum, producing J=2((c+1)K+29).

The point-line theorem is a legitimate imported interface in an eleven-page specialist account. Its hypotheses q≥10⁶D³ and θ≤1/(4·10⁶), arbitrary oriented rows and allowance of zero directions are present. Its conclusion gives field distance at most 2θ; encoding halves that distance, and adding the primary 28κ gives Sκ with S=28+J. The short text properly attributes the explicit constants to Lee's proof rather than to ALMSS. The bivariate and ruled-surface paragraphs orient the reader but do not prove the imported theorem in full. The long sections contain the omitted error-locator, crossing and exceptional-slice arguments; those regions were read, while their entire Lean dependency closure was not. [Short lines 208–241; long §§4–5; EncodedLineLowDegreeTest.lean, PointLineSoundness.lean and EncodedLineLowDegreeSoundness.lean.]

### Rejection-dependent correction is the best central explanation to strengthen

The introduction to §5 identifies the right obstacle: global average proximity does not protect the special subsets (x,0) and (x,Z_H(x)). The corrector fixes a target a, samples v uniformly and z uniformly from F×, tests that auxiliary row, compares its corrected value at z with the primary table, and returns its corrected value at zero if the tests pass. The same homogeneous label and the appropriate shared inner correction direction belong in this description. The claim is pointwise in a and averaged over that random label and the remaining seed. Its failure event is **rejection or a wrong returned bit**, not error conditional on acceptance and not a guarantee for every fixed label.

Let R_a be its actual rejection probability and e=(c+1)KR_a. Inner decoding costs at most KR_a because inner-test failure forces rejection. Each of the two corrected auxiliary values can be replaced by its decoded line value at cost e. For decoded T_v, the missing concise inequality is

\[
\Pr_{v,L}[L(T_v(0))\ne L(P(a))]
\le
\Pr_{v,z,L}[L(T_v(z))\ne L(P(a+zv))]
+\frac{D}{2(q-1)}.
\]

For a direction with an incorrect field value at zero, the difference polynomial is nonzero and has at most D roots among the q−1 nonzero parameters. Averaging a homogeneous label detects every nonzero field difference with probability one half. For a direction with the correct origin value, the left contribution is zero. This proves the inequality without demanding that each individual label detecting the origin also detect the sampled point.

The sampled comparison contributes e+R_a; replacing its primary bit contributes δ because a+zv is uniform for every nonzero z. Replacing the returned value contributes another e, and the explicit rejection part of the failure event contributes another R_a. The result is (2+2(c+1)K)R_a+δ+D/[2(q−1)], exactly equation (14). This reconstruction supports the statement and suggests R04–R05. The arbitrary auxiliary table is essential: it can cause frequent rejection, but cannot frequently return a wrong bit while seldom rejecting. [Short lines 247–262; BinaryLocalCorrectionSoundness.lean, theorem signature at lines 222–236, proof through line 313, and its preceding lemmas.]

### ZERO and the seven checks really do share the necessary objects

Name the ZERO experiment: choose one x and one homogeneous label L, read the primary bit a_bit, obtain corrected certificate bits b₀ and b_Z at (x,0) and (x,Z_H(x)), and accept exactly when neither correction aborts, b₀=0 and b_Z=a_bit. The calls share x and L and have their prescribed independent remaining seeds. If A fails to vanish on the grid, at least one residual polynomial M(X,0) or M(X,Z_H(X))−A(X) is nonzero; its degree is at most Dh. A single nonzero residual yields ideal rejection at least (1−Dh/q)/2. Independence of the two residual bits is neither needed nor asserted.

Each correction rejection is contained in actual ZERO rejection. Charging the two correction failures and primary error yields equation (15). For the derived cubic read, a fresh-mask replacement changes rejection by at most 2ε, so that error enters inside the multiplier 1+2C in equation (16). The selected reader theorem proves the base inequality (15); DerivedZeroOnGrid.rejectionRate_lower_bound supplies the derived-read version. Add that source route locally. Removing the multiplier, replacing actual fresh randomness by a guessed deterministic oracle, or assuming auxiliary proximity would change the proof. [Short lines 266–290; BinaryZeroOnGridSoundness.lean and DerivedZeroOnGridSoundness.lean.]

In §6, use eight **arbitrary received tables with intended honest contents**. Equation (17) should describe received bits; writing Q_P there makes the difference identity appear assumed before the decoded independent polynomial Q is introduced. The following proof correctly establishes it. Four outer tests choose P,Q,M_A,M_B once, with errors at most ε=Sκ. Three bit replacements cost 3ε. If Q−P(X)+P(Y) were nonzero, its support and half-distance would force too much rejection. The two derived constraints have degrees at most 3D and 5D, so the common ZERO degree budget is 5D. The gap then rules out either grid violation, recovering a proper colouring. The global primary polynomials stay fixed; auxiliary row polynomials may be selected locally by correction proofs and do not need a globally unique decoding. [Short lines 296–338; ThreeColorDecodedSoundness.lean and ThreeColorVerifierSoundness.lean.]

## A concrete improvement: give the reader a compact interface inventory

The successful explanatory simplification is to expose the proof's interfaces before the constants, without changing a test or inserting a new theorem. A small table near §2's final paragraph can name the four primary received tables, their intended functions and the labels used:

| Received primary table | Intended field function | Point domain | Full labels |
|---|---|---|---|
| w_P | Colour extension P | F^m | B₃ |
| w_Q | Difference Q | F^(2m) | B₃ |
| w_A | Certificate M_A | F^(2m) | B₁ |
| w_B | Certificate M_B | F^(4m) | B₁ |

Each has an auxiliary table indexed by an oriented line, an inner point in F^(cb), and a degree-one label. Introduce the label notation fully in §3; the early table can use “cubic” and “affine” in prose until then. An honest proof fills these tables with encodings; soundness starts from arbitrary bits. After decoding, a short ledger can record that the same P,Q,M_A,M_B have both full and homogeneous error at most ε. Full error is spent on derived reads, homogeneous error on weighted comparisons, and correction rejection on the two nonuniform certificate queries. This lets a reader predict which guarantee each next test needs. R07 provides usable text and avoids introducing eight complicated symbols into every subsequent display.

The attempted simplification of dropping restricted proximity failed, as shown above. The successful inventory retains both distances while reducing memory load. It also explains why “Fix these choices” is a load-bearing sentence rather than a dispensable short transition. No new research programme is justified by this review; the outstanding work is exposition, attribution, source navigation and validation of the revised artifacts.
