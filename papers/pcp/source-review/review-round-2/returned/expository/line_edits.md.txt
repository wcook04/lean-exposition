## R02 — Make the characteristic-two calculation available to this audience

**Source:** §4, `pcp-expository-colour-polynomials`, lines 239–267. **Action:** replace the initial field-introduction paragraph, retaining the following constraint table and K4 counterexample.

> We now use a different field from the five-element field in the earlier examples. In the four-element field, every element has the form $a+b\omega$, where $a,b\in\{0,1\}$. Add the coefficients modulo two, and multiply using $\omega^2=\omega+1$. Thus $1+1=0$, subtraction equals addition, and
> $$
> (a+b\omega)(c+d\omega)=(ac+bd)+(ad+bc+bd)\omega.
> $$
> The three nonzero elements are $1,\omega,\omega+1=\omega^2$. Since $\omega(\omega+1)=1$, each has cube one. These are our three colours. If two colours differ, their difference is another nonzero element of this field and therefore also has cube one. If they are equal, their difference is zero and its cube is zero. Consequently $(u-v)^3-1$ is zero exactly for distinct colours.
>
> The full construction uses a larger field of characteristic two containing these same four elements. The colour calculation stays inside this four-element subfield. The two constraints have separate jobs: $u^3-1=0$ restricts a vertex to the three colours, and the edge constraint requires adjacent vertices to have distinct colours. Assigning all four field elements to K4 shows why the first job cannot be omitted.

**Check:** all four cube values and all six distinct colour differences were enumerated. This paragraph assumes the existence of the larger field, supplied later by the parameter choice; it does not pretend to construct arbitrary finite fields.

## R03 — Show why the certificate requests need correction

## R04 — Replace the collapsing digit example

**Source:** §6, `pcp-expository-digit-encoding`, lines 415–440. **Action:** replace the displayed polynomial, its encoding and its numerical substitution together.

> Here is the operation with two base-three digits. Over $\mathbb F_5$, take
> $$
> p(Y)=Y^4+2Y+3.
> $$
> Write an exponent as $j=j_0+3j_1$. The exponents $4,1,0$ have digit pairs $(1,1),(1,0),(0,0)$, respectively. Introduce two groups of variables, $X_{0,0},X_{0,1},X_{0,2}$ and $X_{1,0},X_{1,1},X_{1,2}$, and replace $Y^j$ by $X_{0,j_0}X_{1,j_1}$. This gives
> $$
> \widetilde p=X_{0,1}X_{1,1}+2X_{0,1}X_{1,0}+3X_{0,0}X_{1,0}.
> $$
> Every term now has total degree two. To recover $p(\lambda)$, substitute $(1,\lambda,\lambda^2)$ in the first group and $(1,\lambda^3,\lambda^6)$ in the second. For $\lambda=2$, these groups are $(1,2,4)$ and $(1,3,4)$, with arithmetic modulo five. The encoded polynomial returns
> $$
> 2\cdot3+2\cdot2+3\cdot1=13=3\pmod5,
> $$
> which equals $p(2)$. More generally, each substituted term becomes $\lambda^{j_0+3j_1}=\lambda^j$, so the identity holds for every $\lambda$.
>
> This example has a genuine degree distinction even as a function on the field. A polynomial of degree at most two cannot agree with $p$ at all five field elements: their difference would be a nonzero degree-four polynomial with five roots. The new low-degree table lives on six coordinates; we recover the original one-variable table only on the special substitution points. Those points are not a uniform sample of the new table, which is why correction will be needed there too.

**Why this edit:** the old $Y^5+2Y+3$ is formally degree five but equals the linear function $3Y+3$ at every element of $\mathbb F_5$. Its calculation was correct. The replacement preserves the field and the base-three mechanism while removing that accidental simplification. Retain the next paragraph's distinct, larger degree bound for arbitrary decoded polynomials.

## R05 — Calculate an actual label, then recover it from a corrupted word

**Source:** §7, `pcp-expository-cubic-labels`, lines 604–626; reuse at §9, lines 808–811 and 855–857. **Action:** insert this worked explanation before the existing non-evaluation functional counterexample. The final two paragraphs replace “freshly randomised self-correction” when first used for a derived read; later occurrences can refer back.

> A field value becomes a tuple of bits after we choose a basis. In the four-element example, $u=a+b\omega$ becomes the pair $(a,b)$. Addition is coordinatewise modulo two; the multiplication formula from Section 4 tells us how to multiply pairs. A label is a Boolean polynomial in these coordinates, and the encoded word stores its answer at $u$.
>
> Let $L$ ask for the first coordinate. We want the bit $L(u^3-1)$, without first reading both coordinates of $u$. Applying the multiplication formula twice gives
> $$
> u^3-1=(1+a+b+ab,0).
> $$
> We can therefore ask the single label
> $$
> \Lambda(Z_1,Z_2)=1+Z_1+Z_2+Z_1Z_2.
> $$
> At $u=\omega=(0,1)$, its stored answer is $1+0+1+0=0$, agreeing with $\omega^3-1=0$. In this small field the label simplifies to degree two. The general construction's degree-at-most-three statement remains valid; it is a sufficient bound, not a claim of minimal degree.
>
> This explains an honest read. A corrupted word might be wrong precisely at $\Lambda$, even if it is right at almost every other label. To request $\Lambda$ reliably, choose a fresh uniform polynomial label $R$ of degree at most three and read the two bits
> $$
> g(R)+g(R+\Lambda).
> $$
> Their sum is the desired answer for an honest evaluation word, because evaluation is additive in the label. For the corrupted word, suppose a fraction $e$ of all labels give wrong answers. Both $R$ and $R+\Lambda$ are individually uniform: adding a fixed label permutes the label space. Each read is wrong with probability $e$, so the probability that either is wrong is at most $2e$. The reads need not be independent.
>
> In the full oracle, a row belongs to a point $x$. The decoded polynomial $P$ has already been fixed. Conditional on $x$ and the requested observation, use a fresh mask $R$ in that row; its error bound is $2e_x$. Averaging over the uniformly sampled $x$ gives $2\varepsilon$ when the full-label table distance is at most $\varepsilon$. We have not assumed that every row has small error, or that the derived labels themselves are uniform. The decoded $P$ here must be the same one used in the homogeneous-label checks later.

**Source transfer:** the operation is already explicit in the specialist paper, `pcp-short-hadamard-encoding`, equations $8$–$9$, lines 128–146. Transfer that operation into the worked route; do not require the learner to open the specialist paper to discover it.

**Additional short insertion after the non-evaluation counterexample:**

> An evaluation word also has $H(1)=1$ and $H(AB)=H(A)H(B)$ whenever the labels and their product lie in the allowed degree ranges. These relations explain what the extra consistency checks test. Their quantitative soundness is an imported theorem; these equations alone are not a proof that the tester is robust.

## R06 — Show a weighted label and distinguish the two averaging domains

**Source:** §8, `pcp-expository-binary-tests`, lines 654–704. **Action:** retain the existing weighted identity, add the short calculation, then put the two domains before the common-witness theorem.

> Multiplying a field value changes the question we ask it. For example, multiplication by $\omega$ sends $(a,b)$ to $(b,a+b)$. If $L$ asks for the first coordinate, then $L(\omega u)$ asks for the second coordinate of $u$. The verifier can use that new label without reading $u$ first. Nonzero multiplication permutes the homogeneous linear labels, preserving their uniform distribution.

| Label family | What an address specifies | Later use |
|---|---|---|
| All degree-at-most-$r$ reduced polynomial labels | A coefficient for every allowed squarefree monomial, including the constant monomial | Random-mask correction of derived polynomial observations |
| Homogeneous linear labels | A parity of the coordinates, with no constant term | Weighted sums, difference checks and outer correction |

> A guarantee averaged over the first family does not automatically hold on the second. With three coordinate bits and degree-three labels, there are eight squarefree monomials and therefore 256 polynomial labels. Only eight labels are homogeneous linear. Start with the encoding of message $101$, and on those eight positions replace its answers by those for $100$. Four bits change. The whole word is only $1/64$ away from the original encoding, but its homogeneous-linear portion is $1/2$ away. This is why the theorem must give both distance bounds.
>
> It must also give them for one polynomial $P$. Choosing a separate polynomial for each family would let the derived constraint and the difference check describe different colourings. For certificate rows, whose full degree bound is one, the first family includes all affine labels; the second family still excludes their constant term.

**Keep immediately after this explanation:** the exact combined theorem, including $D=cb^c$, $Q\ge10^6D^3$, $E\kappa\le1/(4\cdot10^6)$, and both distances at most $S\kappa$ for the same $P$. The expansions of $K,E,S,C$ may move to a nearby parameter note; their definitions and hypotheses may not disappear.

## R07 — Give the outer corrector's operation before its error bound

**Source:** §8, lines 706–727. **Action:** replace the introductory description; retain the exact displayed bound and its qualifications. The final derivation below can be an immediately adjacent worked note.

> Fix the point $a$ whose value is requested. Choose a uniform direction $v$, a uniform nonzero parameter $z$, and a uniform homogeneous label $L$. Independently choose the inner tester’s seed and a uniform direction in the digit-coordinate space. The proof supplies an auxiliary row intended to describe the polynomial on the line $a+Yv$.
>
> First test that auxiliary row with the inner tester. Reject if it fails. Otherwise use the inner corrector to obtain the line's proposed observation at $z$, and compare it with the primary-table bit at $a+zv$. Reject if the observations differ. If they agree, return the inner-corrected observation at zero. This output is intended to be $L(P(a))$. The two inner corrections share this inner direction and the label $L$, on the same chosen outer line. The inner direction is a different object from the outer direction $v$; the two corrections need not be independent.
>
> The random comparison point is uniform in the whole ambient space, even though $a$ was fixed: for every nonzero $z$, the map $v\mapsto a+zv$ is a bijection. This lets the global homogeneous-distance bound control the primary-table comparison. The auxiliary rows can still be arbitrary; testing them is part of this procedure.
>
> Let $R_a$ be the actual probability of rejection. Decode each auxiliary row to a proxy line polynomial $T_v$ of degree at most $D$. Since an inner-test failure forces rejection, the inner correction theorem bounds each correction-to-proxy discrepancy, averaged over the choices, by $e=(c+1)K R_a$. The checked comparison therefore gives a proxy comparison-error bound $e+R_a+\delta$, where $\delta$ is the primary table's homogeneous distance from the already fixed $P$.
>
> If $T_v(0)\ne P(a)$, then $T_v(Y)-P(a+Yv)$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $D$. It has at most $D$ roots among the $Q-1$ nonzero choices of $z$. A uniform homogeneous label detects each nonzero field difference with probability $1/2$. Moving from the comparison point to zero thus costs at most $D/[2(Q-1)]$. Replacing the proxy answer at zero costs another $e$, and counting rejection as failure costs another $R_a$. In total,
> $$
> \Pr[\text{reject or return a wrong bit}]
> \le 2e+2R_a+\delta+\frac{D}{2(Q-1)}
> =C R_a+\delta+\frac{D}{2(Q-1)}.
> $$
> The probability includes the random label. It does not promise this bound for every prescribed label. Nor does it assert that $R_a$ is small: after averaging over the targets requested by ZERO, the corrector’s rejection rate is bounded by ZERO’s observed rejection rate.

**Preserved premises:** $c,b>0$, $D\ge cb^c$, $Q>2c$, full-generator nodes for the tester and valid distinct nonzero weighted nodes for the corrector, primary homogeneous proximity to one $P$, arbitrary auxiliary data. Honest success for every seed requires honest auxiliary line data. The formal whole-field wrapper's zero-parameter acceptance factor belongs to §10 and is not silently removed by this conditional nonzero-parameter description.

## R08 — Derive the soundness comparison as events

**Source:** §9, `pcp-expository-assembly`, lines 838–863. **Action:** replace the explanation after the difference identity with this chain and keep the existing field-size and strict-margin choices.

> Fix the four polynomials supplied by the decoding theorems. We compare random tests using the same random choices. If an ideal test rejects while an actual test accepts, some replaced answer must have changed the decision. Therefore
> $$
> \Pr[\text{ideal rejects}]
> \le \Pr[\text{actual rejects}]
>   +\Pr[\text{some replacement changes the decision}].
> $$
> This is an event inclusion and a union bound; it requires no independence between the possible errors.
>
> First replace the freshly masked derived constraint read by the exact bit of the constraint built from the fixed colour polynomial. The bound from Section 7 is $2\varepsilon$. Call the resulting ZERO procedure $Z'$. Its rejection probability is at most $\kappa'=\kappa+2\varepsilon$. We average over the original fresh mask in this comparison; we do not fix a favourable mask and call it a deterministic oracle.
>
> In $Z'$, each of the two certificate-corrector rejections also rejects the enclosing procedure. Each actual corrector rejection rate is consequently at most $\kappa'$, after averaging over the enclosing choices. With certificate distance at most $\varepsilon$, the two correction bounds give
> $$
> \Pr[\text{ideal ZERO rejects}]
> \le \kappa'+2\left(C\kappa'+\varepsilon+
>             \frac{5D}{2(Q-1)}\right).
> $$
> Here $5D$ is a common safe degree bound: the vertex constraint has degree at most $3D$, the edge constraint at most $5D$, and both certificate polynomials at most $D$. A false grid condition supplies a nonzero residual of degree at most $5Dh$; the root bound and the random homogeneous label give ideal rejection at least $(1-5Dh/Q)/2$. Substitution yields
> $$
> \frac{1-5Dh/Q}{2}
> \le (1+2C)(\kappa+2\varepsilon)+2\varepsilon+
>       \frac{5D}{Q-1}.
> $$
> In particular, the factor $1+2C$ multiplies the derived-read error too. The correction bound uses rejection measured inside $Z'$; small correction rejection has not been assumed separately.

**Tradeoff:** this adds an intermediate procedure and one inequality to remove an otherwise hidden inference. Preserve the existing lower bound $3/8$, strict upper margin $<3/16$, Question 5, and the explanation that decoded objects exist in the proof but are not reconstructed by the verifier.

## R09 — Provide a reconstruction answer without claiming an independent first attempt

**Source:** §11, lines 1154–1165. **Action:** move the task itself before the answers; place a solution after it or behind a disclosure in the reader. This review's solution was produced after seeing Section 11 and checking source support.

> The certificate identity $M(X,0)=0$ asks about a thin slice of a larger table. Global proximity alone permits every point on that slice to be wrong. We fix the certificate polynomial selected by the low-degree theorem and use the outer corrector to request its value there. The corrector tests the supplied line row and compares at a random nonzero point before returning the origin observation; it may reject. Its failure bound refers to actual rejection, the global homogeneous distance and a root term. Because a correction rejection rejects ZERO too, the observed ZERO rejection controls the first term after averaging over ZERO’s requested targets. Coupling the ideal and actual ZERO tests then bounds the ideal rejection. If the decoded constraint did not vanish, one residual would be a nonzero low-degree polynomial, forcing a larger ideal rejection than this bound allows. The same decoded polynomial and the random-label average are necessary at each step.

**Marking guide:** require the thin-slice obstruction, one fixed decoded witness, arbitrary auxiliary data, reject/return operation, marginal uniformity, homogeneous-label averaging, rejection containment and the ideal-versus-actual event inequality. A response that merely says “self-correction fixes the errors” has not reconstructed the argument. No additional transfer question is necessary before the existing task is made usable.
