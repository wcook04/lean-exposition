# Review of the worked PCP exposition

## 1. Judgment and evidence boundary

The current exposition has repaired the main quantitative omissions identified in the supplied earlier reviews. It states the simultaneous decoding guarantee, the outer corrector's rejection-aware estimate, the difference-check inequality, the ZERO inequality and compatible field parameters. Those are present facts of this edition. Repeating the old complaint that the soundness contract is missing would be an inaccurate review. The source audit also supports their delicate qualifications. The strongest new criticism is that the reader is shown several correct contracts before being shown the operations that make those contracts intelligible. [Primary §§8–10, lines 667–968; retrospective comparison in `support/prior_comparison.md`.]

For a specialist, the paper is a coherent compact reconstruction conditional on its imported testing theorems. For the declared reader—polynomial arithmetic, finite sums and elementary probability—it still leaves substantial work at the changes of representation: characteristic-two field arithmetic; a basis as a tuple of bits; a polynomial label as an address; a corrected derived bit as two actual reads; and the coupling of actual and ideal verifier decisions. These are located pedagogical gaps. I did not observe human learners failing at them. The first-reading document records a model's attempt, with its advantages and exposure stated honestly. [Audience statement, lines 35–40; §§4, 7–9; `first_reading.md`.]

The return contains separate judgments because three different questions must not collapse into one: is the mathematics stated faithfully, can the promised learner perform the intermediate operations, and does the reader interface make the supporting source reachable? The first has strong local support, the second needs revision, and the third has useful navigation plus a concrete desktop layout defect. No aggregate score would express that combination accurately.

## 2. Strongest theorem and proof spine recovered

For each language $L$ in NP, the account asserts one verifier and one implementation, with constants and a polynomial fixed for $L$. On an input of length $n$, it uses exactly $a\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil+b$ fair random bits, makes at most a fixed number of proof-bit queries and reads only below a polynomial address bound. A yes-instance has one proof accepted on every seed; every proof for a no-instance is accepted with probability at most $1/2$. The proof is fixed before randomness. Polynomial input computation is compatible with constantly many proof-bit queries. The converse guesses the bounded prefix, zero-extends it to the oracle model and enumerates every seed in polynomial time. These quantifiers and the runtime requirement match the inspected source after expanding the named definitions. [§2, lines 86–137; `PCP.np_subset_pcp`, `PCP.pcp_subset_np_concrete`; the exact signatures are in `claim_checks.json`.]

The following is the proof spine I can reconstruct after the first reading and source comparison. Its imported components are identified rather than presented as consequences of small examples.

1. **Expose the need for encoding.** K4 with disjoint triangles remains uncolourable while the fraction of bad original edges can tend to zero. A fixed number of uniformly sampled original edges cannot provide the desired constant gap. The theorem changes the proof representation. [§1.]
2. **Turn a colouring into polynomial conditions.** On a grid, the vertex constraint restricts values to the three cube roots, and the edge constraint separates adjacent colours. The public adjacency polynomial is computed from the graph. Its values do not come from untrusted advice. [§4.]
3. **Replace vanishing on a grid by two certificate identities.** The quotients from coordinatewise division can be packed in $M(X,U)$. The conditions $M(X,0)=0$ and $M(X,Z_H(X))=P(X)$ together imply grid vanishing even when a proposed certificate has a different form from the honest one. [§5.]
4. **Encode expensive objects.** Base-$b$ digits turn honest line polynomials into constant-degree multivariate polynomials. Binary polynomial-label words make observations of field expressions available through bit queries. Soundness allows arbitrary decoded inner polynomials and therefore uses the larger bound $D=cb^c$. [§§6–7.]
5. **Import local-testing and decoding contracts.** Weighted testing and Hadamard consistency lead to one low-degree polynomial close on both full and homogeneous labels. The point-versus-line theorem is a genuine substantial imported theorem; the elementary examples do not prove it. [§§6, 8.]
6. **Correct special-point requests.** A global distance estimate does not control a thin slice or a specially constructed label. Fresh random masks handle prescribed labels. The outer corrector handles prescribed geometric points, with an output that may be rejection. Its theorem is controlled by actual rejection, primary distance and a root term. [§§7–8.]
7. **Fix four decoded polynomials and combine seven checks.** Four low-degree tests, a difference check and two ZERO checks all refer to these choices. Small total rejection forces the exact difference identity and both grid conditions; these recover a colouring. [§9.]
8. **Close resource and gap claims.** The chosen field, grid, labels and addresses have polynomial size and logarithmic descriptions; the implementation has a separate runtime proof. The positive constant gap survives the formal sampling wrapper. Independent repetition on a fixed proof lowers soundness to one half, and seed enumeration proves the converse. [§10.]

This account makes clear where the difficulty lives. The interpolation and certificate algebra are not the deepest imported result. The learner's hardest composition step is understanding how average guarantees remain usable when the verifier requests constrained points and labels, and how all those requests continue to describe the same witness. The strongest mathematical feature of the exposition is that it already preserves that boundary in its formal statements.

## 4. Teaching assessment by mechanism

### The opening and algebraic constraints

The growing graph family is an effective entry. A single finite graph would show only one bad fraction; adjoining triangles shows why a constant sampling budget fails uniformly. The opening also correctly distinguishes reading the input from reading the proof. The resource words need a short definition at first use, not a new complexity prerequisite. R01 makes that local repair. [§§1–2; PA-01, PA-02, PA-03.]

The five-element interpolation example prepares polynomial evaluation and division, but it does not teach arithmetic in characteristic two. The first substantial prerequisite jump is the introduction of $\omega$, its powers and the colour predicate. The relevant operations can be taught cheaply: represent F4 by pairs of bits, state $\omega^2=\omega+1$, compute multiplication once, and show that the three nonzero elements all have cube one. This both explains the edge residual and prepares the later binary coordinates. R02 gives the complete calculation. The K4 assignment using all four field elements must remain: it is a precise counterexample to omitting the vertex restriction. [§4, lines 239–267.]

The certificate section has an especially good choice of exercise. Its reverse implication is visible at the grid because $Z_H(x)=0$, and the failed one-identity variant is elementary. The degree accounting needs its own sentence: substituting the degree-$h$ polynomial can inflate a total-degree bound by $h$. The prose should not leave that factor to appear for the first time in a later soundness inequality. The thin-slice issue deserves the small $25/625$ example in R03, because it turns “special points” into a conditional-distribution problem the declared audience already understands. [§5, lines 322–377; §9, lines 838–852.]

### The digit encoding example needs a scope repair

The current F5 example is correct as formal polynomial substitution: its encoded polynomial evaluates to the original polynomial. But every field element satisfies $y^5=y$, so $Y^5+2Y+3$ is already the linear function $3Y+3$ on that field. The example does not demonstrate that the evaluation function required the advertised higher degree. That is a teaching weakness, not a counterexample to the encoding theorem. [§6, lines 415–440.]

R04 keeps F5 and replaces the polynomial by $Y^4+2Y+3$. Its base-three digits give a degree-two polynomial on six coordinates, and its value at two is three. Exhaustive evaluation checks the substitution identity at every field element; enumeration excludes all 125 quadratic representatives. The root bound explains the exclusion without relying on the program. This example also makes the next obstruction visible: substitution accesses a special subset of the six-dimensional table, so ordinary global agreement is insufficient. That is a useful connection between two consecutive mechanisms.

Do not simplify away the paragraph on arbitrary decoded inner polynomials. An honest encoding has a special multilinear form; soundness must allow the received table to decode to a different degree-$c$ polynomial. The inflated $D$ is meaningful negative knowledge: it states exactly what testing has not established. [§6, lines 450–462.]

### Binary labels require one complete worked query

The eight-bit word, the corrupted bit and the failed evaluation functional are good anchors. “Homogeneous linear functional” should first be identified as a parity with no constant term. More substantially, naming a basis and saying a cubic expression is a label does not let this audience manufacture the address. R05 reuses the same F4 pair representation to compute $u^3-1=(1+a+b+ab,0)$ and the actual label $1+Z_1+Z_2+Z_1Z_2$. The simplification to degree two is explicitly scoped. [§7, lines 532–536, 604–634.]

The next missing bridge is between an honest single-label read and the $2\varepsilon$ error later charged to a received oracle. Display $g_x(R)+g_x(R+\Lambda_{x,L})$. Fix the point and requested label, then draw a fresh uniform full label $R$. The two read positions are individually uniform, even though dependent. Conditional row error is at most twice that row's distance; averaging over uniform points gives the global estimate. This avoids two invalid shortcuts: assuming every row is individually close, or assuming the map that constructs the desired label preserves uniformity. The specialist paper already contains this operation in equations (8)–(9); its omission from the worked route is a transfer failure, not a new mathematical problem. [Primary §9, lines 808–811, 855–857; short lines 128–146; `HadamardDerivedOracle.lean` 31–195, selected regions.]

The full/homogeneous distinction likewise benefits from a calculation. The checked 256-label example has distance $1/64$ on all labels but $1/2$ on the eight homogeneous labels. Thus the restricted guarantee is substantive. The theorem also has to select one $P$ for both guarantees; a separate witness for each would break assembly. The current exposition states this correctly. R06 gives the reader a reason for both clauses before the constants arrive. [§8, lines 667–704.]

## 5. Hard-step reconstruction: correction and the ZERO budget

Here is the part that should determine whether the next revision is a worked exposition. A reader should be able to say what the corrector samples, reads, rejects and returns, then account for the soundness losses. The following reconstruction is source-assisted, after the initial reading.

Fix a target $a$, a primary oracle close in homogeneous distance $\delta$ to a degree-$D$ polynomial $P$, and arbitrary auxiliary rows for lines through $a$. Sample a uniform line direction, a nonzero parameter and a homogeneous label, an independent inner-test seed and a uniform inner direction in the digit-coordinate space. The two inner corrections share that inner direction and the label. Test the auxiliary row; if the test fails, reject. Correct its value at the nonzero parameter and compare with the primary bit at that point; if the comparison fails, reject. Otherwise return its corrected origin bit. All of this is actual verifier behavior. The proof's decoded line polynomial is an analysis object. [Long lines 1137–1180; `BinaryLocalCorrection.lean` and `BinaryLocalCorrectionSoundness.lean` selected regions.]

Write $R_a$ for this procedure's actual rejection. Auxiliary inner-test failures are included in rejection, so decoding and inner correction give an average replacement cost $\alpha=(c+1)K R_a$. The ideal comparison failure is at most $\alpha+R_a+\delta$. For each fixed nonzero parameter, the sampled ambient point is uniform, which warrants the $\delta$ term. If a proxy line gives a wrong field value at zero, its difference from the restriction of $P$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $D$. A random nonzero point can hide that disagreement only on at most $D/(Q-1)$ of the parameters. Averaging homogeneous labels gives the half factor. Moving back to the actual returned bit costs another $\alpha$; including rejection costs another $R_a$. Thus the failure probability is at most

$$
[2+2(c+1)K]R_a+\delta+\frac{D}{2(Q-1)}.
$$

No smallness of $R_a$ or closeness of the auxiliary data was assumed. This is precisely why the theorem can be used inside ZERO. It is also a label-average theorem, not a reliability statement for every prescribed label. R07 gives a manuscript-ready version of this reconstruction.

For ZERO, fix the same decoded colour and certificate polynomials used by the other tests. Replace the derived cubic read with its ideal value; fresh-mask correction changes the outcome with probability at most $2\varepsilon$. The resulting procedure has rejection at most $\kappa'=\kappa+2\varepsilon$. Each certificate-corrector rejection rejects that procedure, so each is controlled by $\kappa'$ after averaging over the targets ZERO requests. An ideal rejection can occur without an actual rejection only if one of the two replacements changes the decision. Using a common safe degree $5D$, the two correction bounds therefore give

$$
\Pr[\text{ideal ZERO rejects}]
\le\kappa'+2\left(C\kappa'+\varepsilon+\frac{5D}{2(Q-1)}\right).
$$

A false grid condition makes one residual nonzero; its degree is at most $5Dh$. The root and half-distance bounds supply the competing lower bound $(1-5Dh/Q)/2$. These yield exactly the displayed inequality in the manuscript. The factor $1+2C$ must multiply the derived-read error as well as $\kappa$. R08 makes that dependency explicit. The current final strict contradiction is correct and must survive the edit. [§9, lines 823–888; short equations (15)–(18); long ZERO analysis.]

An integrated exercise should ask for this chain before showing its solution. Keep the existing reconstruction task, move it before the answer section or place the solution behind a disclosure, and grade for the actual operations. No additional transfer questions are needed before that task is made workable. A response that names “self-correction” but omits the rejection containment or same witness is incomplete. [§11, lines 1154–1165; R09.]

## 6. A serious simplification attempt and why I declined the redesign

I investigated whether cubic labels are unnecessarily expensive in characteristic two. Squaring is F2-linear, and field multiplication is bilinear in binary coordinates. Therefore $u^3=u\cdot u^2$ has coordinate functions of degree at most two. The F4 calculation makes this concrete. For the colour and difference cubics, a degree-two label representation is mathematically available as a function of the input coordinates. This is a real simplification observation, not a suggestion to rename the existing degree-three interface. [R05 and its checked formula; ABSSW v2 `technical_tools.tex`, composition definitions and degree bound, lines 162–188.]

Lowering the pinned implementation's parameter would require proving a new coordinate-level composition lemma and carrying it through the derived oracle, tests, transports, query counts and final runtime/correctness assembly. The generic existing formal theorem uses the ordinary polynomial-degree bound. A toy quadratic identity does not supply those new proofs, and the constant-query theorem does not require a smaller constant. The outcome is therefore to **decline the architectural change in this revision**, while explaining in the worked example that degree three is sufficient rather than minimal. This preserves the paper's correspondence to the actual implementation. It is not a proof that no implementation improvement is possible.

A second simplification is editorial and immediately useful: move expanded constant formulas after a role-based exact contract. Keep $D$, the field and rejection thresholds, the same-witness conclusion and the displayed ZERO inequality on the main route. Put the numerical expansions in an adjacent parameter note. This reduces simultaneous symbol handling without changing the theorem. The original detailed reduction compiler likewise belongs in an optional route after its exact reduction contract. No new production workflow or larger source audit is needed to make these moves. [§§8, 10; `repair_plan.md`.]
