# Editorial decisions binding all three PCP papers (third round)

These decisions are the orchestrator's, made from the two scout reports and the reviewer returns. They apply to the full argument (L), the specialist paper (S) and the worked exposition (E). Where a decision conflicts with a reviewer's replacement text, the decision wins; where it conflicts with the mathematical flag ledger, the ledger wins.

## Part 1. Structure and conventions

1. **Stated results are set off, named and numbered**, as in ABSSW, ALMSS and Arora–Barak: `**Theorem 1 (PCP theorem; Arora–Safra, ALMSS).**`, `**Lemma 3.2 (name; source, locator).**`, `**Definition 2.1 (name).**`, each followed where a proof is given by a paragraph beginning `*Proof.*` and ending with `∎` or "This proves the lemma." Number sequentially within top-level sections. Refer to results by number. Equation tags such as (A14), (B25), (16) stay exactly as they are. In E, number only the handful of results a reader must cite (the PCP theorem, the main testing and correction statements); worked examples stay prose with "Example" run-in heads where they already have one.
2. **Nothing is used before it is stated.** L keeps the assembly-first order settled in the second round, but every lemma the assembly uses is stated there as a numbered result with "proved in Section X", so no tag or name appears before its statement. S and E follow the same rule.
3. **One preliminaries or notation section per paper** in L and S, fixing the field, the grid, degrees, distance, the proof oracles and the label spaces once, in the literature's names. E introduces each object at first need, but with the same names and symbols.
4. **Section titles are noun phrases naming the mathematics.** No sentence or question titles, no titles naming a process ("How the proof is organized", "Reading this account", "A reconstruction task and answers"). Question-form run-in paragraph heads inside a section are acceptable, as ABSSW uses them. Because headings are link targets, list every renamed heading (old text, new text) in the notes; the orchestrator updates links in the other papers and the mapping files.
5. **One roadmap.** The introduction ends with a single paragraph on how the paper is organised. Other roadmaps and recaps go, except a one-sentence orientation at the start of a long proof.
6. **Front matter** as in the SK set: title; an italic byline; the credit line for ABSSW and Troy Lee; one short disclosure sentence that the account was prepared with AI assistance and is not an independent expert's acceptance; then a labelled **Abstract**. Nothing else about review, versions or tooling appears before the closing section.
7. **Formalization commentary has a fixed place.** The papers explain mathematics. Where Lee's formalization proves a variant that differs from ABSSW (sampling from all of the field, ten queries, affine labels, a fair coin between components, weaker field-size hypothesis, explicit constants), say so once in a paragraph headed `*Remark (formalization).*` directly after the statement concerned. Do not narrate the formalization inside proofs. Lean pointers: in L, each numbered result ends with one line `Lean: ...` carrying its existing links; inline mid-sentence Lean links move there. In S and E, Lean links live in the closing section only, plus the `Remark (formalization)` paragraphs. No Lean link or reference definition is deleted.
8. **One closing section** titled "Formalization and sources" in each paper, holding: what the Lean proves (the three endpoint declarations), the differences from ABSSW collected in one place, credits (including Lee's identification of the error in version 1, and the earlier literature), and the short statement of what was and was not checked. E's exercises move to a section titled "Exercises" with "Solutions" after it, placed after the mathematics and before the closing section.
9. **Explicit constants are a contribution of the formalization, so they stay, but out of the flow of the argument.** In the argument state bounds in the form the literature uses, with named constants ($K_c$, $g_c$, ...) or $O_c(\cdot)$ where the literature does. Collect the exact values, the 187-read count with its breakdown, the seed-length formula and the gap in one place per paper (a subsection "Explicit constants" or the existing parameter table). Give an exact rational or a formula once; do not print long decimal expansions in prose.
10. **Tables and lists.** Keep a table only where a reader looks something up: the proof oracles and their address spaces, the parameter choices, the explicit constants, a worked numerical trace. Everything else that is currently a table, bulleted inventory or run of one-line parallel paragraphs becomes argued prose. Algorithm-style numbered steps for a test or the verifier are standard in this literature and are welcome where the paper describes a procedure.
11. **Citations**: one style in all three papers, alphabetic keys with the locator inside the brackets, e.g. `[ABSSW26, Lemma 3.3]`, `[ALMSS98, Theorem 45]`, `[AS98, §5.2]`, `[AB09, §18.4]`, `[Lee26]`, each paper ending with a **References** list in the same key order. Keep every existing cited work and every URL. E gains a reference list.
12. **What is new** gets one honest paragraph in each introduction, as ABSSW's "result and ideas" paragraph does: these papers contribute an explanation; the construction is ABSSW's; the formalization, its explicit constants and its corrected test hypotheses are Lee's; the classical tools are credited by name.
13. **Length ceilings** (packet word count): L 22,300; S 6,150; E 11,300. The structural changes above are paid for by the cuts lens A identified and by removing repeated roadmaps and formalization narration.

## Part 5. Corrections found by the independent reads (override earlier parts)

1. **Plurality correction locator.** Part 2 cites [ABSSW26, Definition 4.4] for the plurality correction. That is wrong: Definition 4.4 there is "Correction for f" (the field value whose Hadamard encoding is closest to a received word), and ABSSW never use the word "plurality". The plurality correction and its pointwise bound $2(c+1)\delta$ are in Kaufman and Sudan [KS08, Lemma 3.1]. Cite that.
2. **The check $f_Q=f_P+f_P$.** ABSSW give it no name (it is "the check in Line 8" of their verifier, analysed in their Claim 7.3). All three papers call it the **difference check**, the name L and E already use, say once what it checks (that the difference table agrees with the colouring table), and do not attribute the name to ABSSW. Do not call it the consistency check.
3. **Citation keys** in all three papers: [AB07] for the Arora and Barak January 2007 draft; [FS95/13] for Friedl and Sudan; [KS08] for Kaufman and Sudan.
4. **Relation words for sources.** Where Lee's statement differs from the cited ABSSW statement in hypotheses, sampling or bound, cite it as "cf. [ABSSW26, ...]" and state the difference in the formalization remark. Known cases: the inner low-degree test (fair coin; ABSSW run both checks), the binary low-degree test ($z$ from all of $F$; ABSSW from $F^\times$), the two corrector calls in a zero-on-grid test (independent seeds; ABSSW share one), the Hadamard test factor 28 with no small-rejection hypothesis (ABSSW Lemma 3.10 gives $\delta$ and $3\delta$ below a threshold), standard local correction (ABSSW Lemma 3.4 needs $q>Cc^3$).
