## 1. What the manuscript does well

The continuous example is its principal explanatory contribution. A single vertex condition from a properly coloured edge is followed through:

1. the colour extension $\chi(X)=1+\omega^2X$;
2. the residual $P(X)=\chi(X)^3-1$;
3. its factorisation by the grid-vanishing polynomial;
4. the certificate $M(X,U)=U(X+\omega^2)$;
5. the line restrictions at the two certificate targets;
6. the digit encoding of those restrictions;
7. the actual weighted bit addresses.

The particular off-grid point is valuable. At $x=\omega$, the colouring extension is zero and the residual is one, while the colouring on the vertex grid remains valid. That prevents a reader from confusing a grid condition with an identity requiring the constraint polynomial to vanish everywhere. The two certificate identities then explain exactly how an ambient-space check can certify a condition on a smaller grid. Keep b0064–b0081 and b0180–b0191 as one connected example.

The counterexamples also earn their space. The diluted $K_4$ shows why uniformly checking graph edges fails. The four-colour assignment to $K_4$ shows why the vertex-validity equation is necessary. The corruption of a zero slice shows why global distance does not control prescribed queries. The fixed-anchor example shows why randomising a line's direction does not justify using an average over all line indices. Question 6 shows why a uniform mask must remain uniform after the request is fixed. These are different failures of different inferences; combining them into a generic warning about corrupt tables would lose mathematical understanding.

The elementary reverse inclusion, the full error-budget calculation, and the small Boolean-to-colouring gadget also belong. They let the reader see how local query operations become a complexity theorem. Appendix A keeps the quantitative existence argument available without forcing all its arithmetic into the main route. These choices preserve settled decisions N1–N7.

## 4. Precision repairs that matter

### The exercise needs the rejection hypothesis

b0237 supplies a table close to a polynomial; b0249 concludes that the polynomial vanishes on a slice. Closeness does not imply vanishing. For example, an exact encoding of $M=1$ has distance zero but satisfies $M(X,0)=1$. The revision explicitly assumes Section 9's parameter and rejection bounds and fixes the polynomial decoded in that argument.

The source supports this repair. `GridVanishing.isCertificate_of_acceptanceRate_gt` requires the nonempty grid, degree bounds and an acceptance threshold before concluding both identities (`formal/lean/PCP/Algebraic/ZeroOnGridCheck.lean`, lines 72–89). The exercise now reconstructs that conditional argument.

### The omitted test is a mixed auxiliary test

b0188 and b0190 call the omitted procedure an inner or syntactic row test. `BinaryLocalCorrection.run` calls `BinaryLowDegreeTest.test`, which chooses between Hadamard row testing and weighted interpolation. The revision uses “auxiliary low-degree test” and states the choice. The eight displayed interpolation reads remain the same, and the omitted queries remain omitted. The correction does not insert a new check or change 187.
