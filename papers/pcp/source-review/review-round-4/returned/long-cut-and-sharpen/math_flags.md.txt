## MF-02: Complete the cofactor determinant explanation

**Location:** b0329.

**Issue:** The sentence “This is a (k+1)-minor and vanishes by maximality” is applied to every original row. If the appended row is already among the selected k rows, the determinant vanishes because it has repeated rows. If it is a new row, maximality of the chosen nonzero minor applies directly. The current prose leaves the first case implicit under the ordinary convention that a minor selects distinct rows.

**Source evidence:** `formal/lean/PCP/Algebraic/PolynomialMatrixKernel.lean:118–125`, `exists_bounded_kernel_of_minor`, requires the appended determinant to vanish for every row. Its construction is proved at lines 126–170. `exists_bounded_kernel_of_maximal_minors`, lines 180–227, obtains that universal condition. The formal predicate P quantifies over arbitrary row maps; maximality there already covers repeated-row maps. The proposed prose spells out the elementary case split for readers using the usual distinct-row meaning of “minor”. It does not repair a missing Lean proof.

**Proposed full block:**

```markdown
The following cofactor construction supplies a nonzero polynomial kernel with a degree bound. Choose a largest nonzero $k$-by-$k$ minor. The empty minor has determinant one, so such a $k$ exists, and $k\le n-1$. Append one column outside its columns. Form a vector supported on these $k+1$ columns whose coordinates are the alternating $k$-by-$k$ cofactors. Its coordinate at the appended column is the chosen nonzero determinant. For any row of the original matrix, the row's product with this vector is the determinant obtained by adjoining that row to the chosen $k$ rows. If a row repeats, this determinant is zero. Otherwise it is a $(k+1)$-minor and vanishes by maximality. Hence the vector is a nonzero kernel vector. Every coordinate is a determinant with $k$ factors of degree at most $d$, so has degree at most $kd\le(n-1)d$.
```

**Native check:** confirm the row/column order and cofactor sign convention in the prose. Nonzeroness, the kernel identity and the degree bound remain the same.

**Locations:** b0077–b0078 and b0091.

**Issue:** The assembly fixes D=cm^c. Its table then uses D as the generic degree parameter in the correction and derived-ZERO contracts. The derived vertex/edge constraints can have degree up to 5D, as b0090 explicitly states. The application and its displayed inequality already use 5D correctly, but the table's reuse of the symbol hides that substitution.

**Source evidence:** Baseline b0563 and b0592 allow a general comparison degree at least cb^c. `formal/lean/PCP/Algebraic/ThreeColorDecodedSoundness.lean:86–124` separates the four decoded degree bounds from the 5D grid comparison. Its two applications of `vanishesOn_of_rejection_bound`, lines 126–169, explicitly use `(D := 5 * D)`.

**Proposal:** use D_1 >= D only in the last two rows of the local summary table, then explicitly substitute D_1=5D. The later generic theorem statements retain their existing locally bound D. No global renaming is intended, and the displayed inequality at b0092 is unchanged.

## Checked precision clarifications that do not change mathematical content

- **b0059 and b0607:** the fixed-request fresh-mask error is a row-wise bound; the global bound follows after averaging over the uniform outer point. See `HadamardDerivedOracle.lean:58–178`. The replacement retains the existing distribution and coefficient.
- **b0196:** the language-wide `NPCertificate` data consist of the relation, witness bound and checker. See `NPThreeColorRuntime.lean:244–265`. No input-specific choice of program is introduced.

- **b0492:** the exact list length 2r+4 is retained. At r=3 it is ten entries, potentially with repeated addresses. `HadamardTest.lean` separately proves `testQueries_length` and `testQueries_card_le`.

- **b0527:** the threshold case split concerns the inner table's rejection parameter. The zero-polynomial case and its numerical bound are unchanged.
