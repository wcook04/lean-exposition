# Scout: literature writing norms vs the three PCP papers

Abbreviations. **L** = `papers/pcp/long/reasoning-record.md`; **S** = `.../short/paper.md`; **E** = `.../expository/main.md`. **ABSSW** = `packet/literature/abssw-v2/tex/<file>:<line>`. Extractions are in `.../literature/text/`: **ALMSS** = `almss-journal.txt`, **AS** = `arora-safra-jacm.txt`, **AB** = `arora-barak-book.txt`. Line numbers are in the current files. No file was edited. Counts are from grep over the supplied texts only; "no hit" means no hit in those texts.

## 1. Standard genre structure

**Research paper (this area).** ABSSW is the cleanest model, and its shape matches ALMSS/AS.

1. Abstract, one paragraph, states the result and the novelty (`main.tex:42`).
2. Introduction (`intro.tex:6`). It gives background and why a simple proof matters (`:6-11`), the ingredients of earlier proofs (`:13`, `:15`), "Our result and ideas" (`:19`), a plain-language problem statement for the one hard step (`:27`, low-degree test), the new object (`:31`), comparison with the nearest prior work (`:45`), and a single-paragraph "Organization of the paper" (`:53`).
3. Preliminaries (`preliminaries.tex:1`). Notation, the standard verifier definition (`:21`), the **PCP Theorem stated once, set off, attributed** (`:46`, "[ALMSS]"), cited imported results as named theorems with locators (`:87` distance lemmas with four citations; `:250` "Low-Degree Test via Lines Table, [ALMSS, Theorem 45]"), and the definition of the lines table (`:103-117`).
4. Technical sections titled by mathematical content: "Two Encodings" (`technical_tools.tex:1`), "Low-Degree Test over Binary Alphabet" (`low_degree_test.tex:1`), "Local Correction over Binary Alphabet" (`local_correction.tex:1`), "Zero-on-Grid Test over Binary Alphabet" (`zero-on-variety_test.tex:1`), "The PCP Verifier" (`pcp-verifier.tex:1`). Each one has the same internal rhythm. A one-paragraph motivation comes first (`local_correction.tex:8` "Why does the above local correction suffice?"; `technical_tools.tex:162` "Why degree-r?"). Then "Expected Oracles and Queries" (`low_degree_test.tex:28`), the algorithm, a numbered Theorem (`low_degree_test.tex:219`), and a "Proof Overview" (`:254`).
5. Proofs of the long lemmas are moved to appendices (`main.tex:77-83`), which keeps the main text readable. A full verifier and honest prover are given once as pseudocode (`appendix-full-verifier.tex:1`).
6. Acknowledgments and references (`main.tex:63-66`).

ALMSS follows the same order: 1 Introduction, 1.2 "Our results" with numbered Theorem 4 (`almss-journal.txt:234-262`), 2 "Proof of the Main Theorem: Overview" (`:289`), 4 "Terminology" (`:765`), technical sections, "7.2 Lines representation of polynomials: Testing and correcting" (`:1410`) with "7.2.1 Low degree test" and "7.2.2 Correcting Polynomials" (`:1429`, `:1497`), and 8.1 "Future Directions" (`:2160`). AS: Introduction (`arora-safra-jacm.txt:53`), Overview (`:396`), technical sections, "5. Two Lemmas About Polynomials" (`:1905`), "6. Discussion" (`:2377`).

**Textbook exposition.** AB ch. 18 states the definition and theorem first ("Definition 18.1 (r,q)-verifier" and "Theorem 18.2 (PCP Theorem [AS98, ALM+98])", `arora-barak-book.txt:17706-17722`), then motivates, then teaches each tool in a section headed "Tool: ..." (18.4.1 "Tool: Linearity Testing and the Walsh-Hadamard Code", `:18211`). A tool section runs: define the code and its distance, state the natural test, state a numbered theorem (18.23, `:18256`), and give a local decoder with a two-line analysis. It closes with "Chapter notes and history" and "Exercises" (TOC `:520-535`). The local decoder for Walsh–Hadamard (`:16899-16915`) is a short boxed procedure with a three-line analysis, not an inventory.

## 3. What the literature does that our papers do not

1. **A single notation/preliminaries section fixing symbols once.** ABSSW `preliminaries.tex:1-100` fixes q=2^t, F_q, P_d(m,F_q), delta, delta-close, oracle notation, F_2[Z]^{<=i}, omega. ALMSS section 4 "Terminology" (`almss-journal.txt:765`). Ours: S has none; L fixes notation separately per chapter (L:98, 723, 1394); E introduces symbols in prose as it goes.
2. **Set-off, numbered theorem statements with citation of the imported source in the heading.** ABSSW `preliminaries.tex:87` (four attributions in the heading), `:250` (named theorem and number). S imports the point-versus-line contract in running text (S:257-265) and attributes it in a following paragraph.
3. **A proof overview that sits between statement and proof**, in the main text, before the technical lemmas. ABSSW `low_degree_test.tex:254` ("Proof Overview: ... a simulation of TabLDT via encodings Psi and H_r"). Ours: the overview is the chapter intro (L:98) and S:36; neither says, per theorem, what the proof does.
4. **Motivation of each new object by the task it solves, before its definition.** ABSSW `intro.tex:31` poses the encoding problem as a displayed task (`local_correction.tex:3-5` italic "task" box), then defines. S:30-36 states the obstacle but then defines objects (S:115, 143) under different names from the task.
5. **A comparison-with-prior-work paragraph and a result-and-ideas paragraph.** ABSSW `intro.tex:19`, `:45`. L/S/E credit sources in a header line and in the closing section (S:453, L:1975) but never say what is novel in the formalization relative to the paper beyond "explicit constants".
6. **Acknowledgments, references, and for textbooks "Chapter notes and history" and exercises** (AB TOC `:520-535`). Ours have credits and sources (S:448, E:1613) but E's exercises are inside the argument (E:1293).
7. **Closing remarks / future directions** (ALMSS `:2160`, AS `:2377`). No hits for future, open problem, discussion in L/S/E.
8. **Algorithm boxes for tests and the verifier** (ABSSW `low_degree_test.tex`, `local_correction.tex:27+`, `appendix-full-verifier.tex`). The seven checks are described only in prose (L:174, S:109, E:1023-1032).
9. **Standard-name statements of what the test is**: "Syntactic Test" with a displayed task (`technical_tools.tex:242-246`), "Self-Correction" (`:417`). Ours rename these (section 4).
