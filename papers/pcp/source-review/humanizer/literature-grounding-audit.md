Scope: read-only comparison of `before/long/reasoning-record.md`, `before/short/paper.md` and `before/expository/main.md` with downloaded v1/v2 and primary source passages. These findings concern attribution and claim scope; they are not a new build or proof verification. PDF page numbers below count the downloaded PDF's physical pages unless a printed number is given.

## Actionable findings

1. **Sharp BLR distance bound needs BCHKS attribution**: the expository manuscript around line 560 calls distance at most rejection the general BLR theorem; the short manuscript around line 187 cites only BLR [4] for the same quantitative assertion. The original BLR author's manuscript §4.2 develops generic group linear self-testing and correction. Bellare–Coppersmith–Håstad–Kiwi–Sudan, *Linearity Testing in Characteristic Two*, author manuscript [Theorem 1.2](https://www.csc.kth.se/tcs/projects/publications/bchks.pdf), PDF p.7 (printed p.5), proves the characteristic-two rejection ≥ distance bound by Fourier analysis. The primary v2 paper, Appendix B, Theorem B.1, PDF p.49, explicitly attributes its stated bound to both BLR93 and BCHKS96. Suggested fix: cite both at the quantitative assertion; distinguish the eight-point corrupted-word example as this exposition's own teaching calculation. The long bibliography already names both and its Walsh proof is correctly scoped.

2. **Weighted low-degree-test lineage belongs at the point of use**: long algebra passage `pcp-long-algebra-weighted-plurality` begins the general weighted averaging proof without a nearby literature anchor; the short manuscript's weighted section around lines 150–174 similarly lacks one. Primary v2 §3.2, immediately after Lemma 3.3, PDF p.15, says the analysis follows roughly Rubinfeld–Sudan 1996 Theorem 4.1 and is a special case of Kaufman–Sudan 2008 Theorem 2.9. The downloaded KS conference manuscript, PDF p.5, states its theorem for linear-invariant families possessing a **2-ary independent** local formal characterization; its §3, PDF pp.6–8, uses plurality and a double-sampling grid. Cite those sources as antecedents and state the qualifying conditions; do not assert that arbitrary nodes and weights automatically meet KS's hypothesis. Identify the explicit generator nodes, Lagrange coefficients, stronger finite-field hypotheses and constants as the corrected v2/Lean form. The long bibliography currently calls it a weighted affine-constraint specialization, but this contextual statement is more useful at the proof itself.

3. **Separate exact-line characterization from the weighted one-line test**: Friedl–Sudan, arXiv:1307.3975v1 (2013 corrected reissue of the 1995 proceedings paper), Lemma 4, PDF p.2, gives the exact-line characterization under `q − q/p − 1 ≥ d` for field characteristic `p`. Primary v2 Appendix A, PDF pp.44–45, invokes that lemma after proving the weighted constraint forces low degree on every line. The long account's independent finite-field argument uses `q > 2d`; identify that bound as the formalized/expository proof's stronger sufficient hypothesis, not the verbatim FS theorem. The short manuscript can anchor its exact-line step to FS without implying that FS is the source of the corrected generator-weight construction.

4. **ALMSS 69 has narrower hypotheses and implicit constants than the explicit Lean version**: ALMSS author manuscript Theorem 69, PDF p.48, assumes nearest row/column polynomials, a small unspecified absolute error threshold and a field of size at least an unspecified constant times `d³`; it concludes bidegree at most `d` and distance at most `4ε`. The long passage around line 399 uses arbitrary approximating row/column polynomials, `q ≥ 1000d³` and `ε ≤ 1/100`; these are the source-checked Lean reconstruction's explicit formulation, not verbatim ALMSS 69. The arbitrary approximants are compatible with the nearest-polynomial version by optimality, but the explicit constants require the independently reconstructed proof. ALMSS Theorem 45, PDF p.27, similarly uses unspecified constants, `δ≤δ₀`, `d>0`, `|F|≥α(d+1)³`, and conclusion `2δ`. Arora–Safra 1998 journal PDF §5.2: Lemma 5.2.2 PDF p.39 / printed p.108; Berlekamp–Welch Lemma 5.2.5 PDF p.41 / printed p.110; Lemma 5.2.7 PDF p.42 / printed p.111. The long manuscript already links this lineage; ensure nearby wording marks the concrete quantitative theorem and arbitrary approximants as the Lean variant.

5. **Textbook edition and examples**: The actual Arora–Barak book file identifies itself as a January 2007 web draft. Its PCP treatment is Chapter 18, §18.1, Definition 18.1, printed p.18.3 (PDF p.353); §18.4 includes Walsh–Hadamard/linearity testing. The expository manuscript's further-reading note correctly flags edition-numbering differences, but naming this chapter and definition helps readers navigate without implying the published-edition numbering. Small F₄ and eight-bit calculations in the explanatory manuscript are pedagogical instances; cite primary general identities where relevant, but do not suggest those worked numbers appear in original sources without verification.
