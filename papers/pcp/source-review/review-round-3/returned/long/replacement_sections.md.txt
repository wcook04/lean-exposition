## 2. Bring the verifier forward: objects, contracts and assembly

### The objects the verifier will test

Let `F` be a characteristic-two field of cardinality `Q` containing a primitive cube root of unity. Let `H⊂F` be the nonempty finite grid alphabet of size `h`, and embed the graph vertices in `H^d`. The three roots of `Z³−1` are the permitted colours. Interpolation extends a colouring to a polynomial `P`. A second polynomial `Q_*` will be required to equal `P(X)−P(Y)`. With the public adjacency interpolant `E`, the two constraints are

\[
A_P(X)=P(X)^3-1,\qquad
B_P(X,Y)=E(X,Y)\bigl(Q_*(X,Y)^3-1\bigr).
\]

The first must vanish on `H^d`; the second must vanish on `H^{2d}`. The grid-vanishing theorem expresses each condition using a polynomial certificate. Denote the certificates by `M_A(X,Z)` and `M_B(X,Y,Z,W)`. Their extra variables encode the coordinate division witnesses, so they have `2d` and `4d` field coordinates respectively.

The received proof has four primary binary tables and one auxiliary line table for each primary table. A principal entry is a Boolean observation of a claimed field value. The colour and difference tables support degree-three labels, because the verifier must form cubic expressions. The certificate tables need degree-one labels. All auxiliary line tables in this formal variant use degree-one labels after the digit encoding.

| Primary object | Field coordinates | Principal label degree | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Colour `P` | `d` | 3 | Supply the colouring and its cubic vertex constraint |
| Vertex certificate `M_A` | `2d` | 1 | Certify that `A_P` vanishes on the vertex grid |
| Difference `Q_*` | `2d` | 3 | Supply the edge difference and its cubic constraint |
| Edge certificate `M_B` | `4d` | 1 | Certify that `B_P` vanishes on the edge grid |

The verifier performs seven checks on those eight received tables: one encoded-line low-degree test for each primary/auxiliary pair; a corrected grid test for the vertex constraint; a corrected grid test for the edge constraint; and a difference-consistency check. All seven must accept. Their seeds are drawn in the specified product space, including sampled data for branches whose answers may later be unused.

### Why small rejection gives one colouring

In the final construction, `c≥1` is the inner degree, `m≥1` is the digit radix, and `D=cm^c` bounds the four decoded polynomials. Assume `d(h−1)≤D`, so interpolation gives the public adjacency polynomial degree at most `2d(h−1)≤2D`. The constants `S_c` and `C_c` are the decoding and correction constants of B15 and B16. Fix a received proof whose total rejection is `κ`. Each of the four low-degree checks rejects with probability at most `κ`. Under the displayed decoding threshold, choose the four output polynomials once. Write `ε=S_cκ` for their common error bound. The colour and difference polynomials satisfy the full degree-three bounds used by derived cubic queries; all four satisfy the homogeneous-label bounds used by weighted comparisons and correction. Every check below refers to these choices.

If `Q_*(X,Y)−P(X)+P(Y)` were a nonzero formal polynomial of degree at most `D`, the root bound and the exact half-distance of homogeneous encodings would give disagreement at least `(1−D/Q)/2`. Replacing the three received answers by the decoded answers costs at most `3ε`. Thus

\[
\kappa+3\varepsilon<\frac{1-D/Q}{2}
\]

forces the difference identity.

The decoded vertex and edge constraints then have degrees at most `3D` and `5D`. Both can be tested using the common ZERO degree bound `5D`. If either fails to vanish on its grid, the derived-ZERO estimate gives

\[
\frac{1-5Dh/Q}{2}
\le (1+2C_c)(\kappa+2\varepsilon)
      +2\varepsilon+\frac{5D}{Q-1}.
\]

The `2ε` inside the parentheses is the fresh derived-read replacement cost. It enters the rejection argument and is therefore multiplied by `1+2C_c`. The other `2ε` comes from the two certificate-proximity charges. The last term is the nonzero-parameter correction loss. The parameter chapter makes the reverse strict inequality hold, so both constraints vanish. The grid values of `P` are then one proper three-colouring.

For a genuine colouring, interpolation, exact certificate identities and honest encoded line restrictions make every check accept on every seed. The resource chapter chooses one parameter family satisfying both completeness and soundness hypotheses, converts the finite seed space to fair bits, and repeats the same-proof verifier enough times to reach soundness one half.

## 3. Replace the binary chapter's opening transition

An auxiliary line table describes a high-degree univariate polynomial, but a test needs only its value at one selected parameter. Reading all its coefficients would use too many bits. The digit encoding replaces that polynomial by a low-degree polynomial in more coordinates, so a local procedure can recover the selected evaluation with a bounded number of field-value probes. The Hadamard encoding then exposes the required Boolean observations of those field values as individual proof bits.

Soundness must also handle a corrupted encoding. An arbitrary low-degree inner polynomial need not have the special form of an honest digit encoding, so its decoded degree has a larger bound. A received Hadamard word need not be close on homogeneous labels merely because it is close on all labels. We prove decoding statements that keep both metrics for the same object, then use them to test and correct the outer polynomial. The promised interfaces are the encoded-line theorem B15 and the outer-correction bound B16; the following derivations establish their exact hypotheses and losses.

## 6. Pair the exact seed formula with its derivation

Let

\[
b_1=1+t,\quad b_2=1+t+\binom t2,\quad
b_3=1+t+\binom t2+\binom t3
\]

be the coefficient counts of the Boolean label spaces. The degree-one and degree-three Hadamard tests use `3b₁` and `2b₃+2b₁+b₂` seed bits respectively. The inner test on `cm` field coordinates uses

\[
I=1+3tcm+2t+3b_1=4+3tcm+5t
\]

bits. The choice bit and sampled data for both alternatives are counted even when one alternative is unused. Put `u=binom(t,2)` and `v=binom(t,3)` only within the following table. The two ZERO entries initially exclude their nonzero-field draws.

| Branch | Power-of-two seed exponent | Nonzero-field draws |
|---|---|---:|
| Colour low-degree | `2td+4tcm+12t+9+3u+2v` | 0 |
| Vertex-certificate low-degree | `4td+4tcm+10t+7` | 0 |
| Difference low-degree | `4td+4tcm+12t+9+3u+2v` | 0 |
| Edge-certificate low-degree | `8td+4tcm+10t+7` | 0 |

| Edge ZERO | `10td+8tcm+12t+9+u+v` | 2 |

\[
(Q-1)^4\,2^{35td+32tcm+69t+50+8\binom t2+6\binom t3}.

\[
