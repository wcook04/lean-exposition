# Second review of the full argument

**Edition reviewed:** the 44-page *The algebraic PCP theorem: a detailed account of the Lean proof*, authored revision `f992ebd7fb9f16f921ec05c4c3f93f33ad933ad6`, reader revision `f500810cd5de90b100940ea7d7d048f54f3bf7c7`, Lean source pin `a0ce13ed0fa264f051c1278e039596ce65557fa4`. Review completed 15 November 2026. This is an AI mathematical and editorial audit, with three parallel component analyses, not human feedback or a new Lean build. Exact reading and execution scope is in [coverage.md](coverage.md).

## 1. Judgment: keep this account, change its first route

This is a worthwhile paper with a substantially successful mathematical reconstruction. It adds things that Troy's existing theorem-and-definitions guide intentionally does not supply: the polynomial kernel and clean rectangle in bivariate agreement, the treatment of arbitrary nearest-line ties, the four mixed-plane errors, the fixed polynomial in ruled-surface rigidity, simultaneous binary distance guarantees, correction on nonuniform certificate slices, and the concrete composition of soundness and resource bounds. These are reasons to preserve a long account. They are also the places where a source owner can find explanatory work worth checking. [L, 388–725, 890–1320, 1456–1922; G, reading-guide §§7–9.]

I found **no new mathematical blocker in the load-bearing argument inspected**. That conclusion has a defined scope: complete reading of the primary prose, reconstruction of its principal inferences, selected Lean statement and proof-region inspection, exact finite calculations, and comparison with selected original literature. It is not a certification of every dependency, a fresh kernel replay, or a claim that an unfamiliar human can learn the proof unaided. The source and calculation coverage is substantial, but those evidence classes must remain distinct.

The current paper is strongest as a sequential proof record. It is less successful at the second job advertised in the brief: allowing a reader to enter one difficult result, recover exactly its interface, and return to the global argument. The eight tables first appear on PDF page 30. The first two pages do give a precise endpoint and a useful roadmap; it would be unfair to say that the construction has no motivation. What they do not give is a usable version of the verifier and its mathematical obligations. A reader accumulates nearly the whole algebraic and binary development before seeing exactly how those obligations fit together. [L, 13–69, 1394–1433; PDF p.30.]

The right next edition is therefore a **complete account with the assembly and import contracts presented first**, followed by the existing detailed proofs. This can be achieved by moving and compressing material already written. It does not require a new proof, a new survey, an expansion of the entire compiler, or replacement of the long paper by the specialist companion. In particular, do not delete the algebra to make the opening faster: the algebra is much of the new explanatory value.

The repair audit is favorable but not unanimous. Of the forty original findings, I judge eighteen fixed, eighteen retained correctly, two addressed by successful alternatives, and two not fixed. The unresolved items are the incomplete generator-theorem selection, F09, and the companion-click behavior reproduced in the live reader, F39. The latter verdict is scoped to the tested deployment and desktop session; I did not successfully execute the frozen static build. Neither finding refutes the mathematical argument. [repair_audit.json; support/browser_observations.json.]

The earlier recommendation of a bounded local revision was reasonable for the defects it found. After reading this edition, I would go one step further on organization. The additional explanations make the local proofs better, but they do not by themselves provide the two promised reading routes. The next change should address that shared cause rather than add another explanatory paragraph at every interface.

## 2. The endpoint and its legitimate boundary

The opening gets the quantifiers right. For each language in the concrete certificate class, choose a verifier, its implementation, constants and a proof-address polynomial before choosing the input. A yes-instance has one proof accepted on every random seed. A no-instance has no proof accepted on more than half the seeds. The proof is fixed before randomness, and the address bound covers every answer branch. These qualifications are essential both to PCP soundness and to the reverse inclusion by checking a finite proof prefix. They should survive any reorganization. [L, 15–42; F/Verifier/Definition.lean; F/Theorem.lean.]

The exact fair-bit schedule is also meaningful. A bare assertion that some seed-length function is bounded by a logarithm would not, by itself, ensure that the function is computable. An arbitrary unary predicate could be encoded in whether the supplied seed has length zero or one, and a zero-query algorithm could inspect that length. The canonical formula prevents this extra information channel. The present opening already says that the formula makes the budget computable, so the example is an optional improvement, not a missing theorem hypothesis. [L, 25, 42; F/Verifier/Definition.lean, `CanonicalRandomness`; G, reading-guide discussion of exact randomness.]

The account proves the mathematical testing arguments and the high-level NP reduction, while importing proved implementation and machine-simulation results from the development. That is a defensible scope. The current implementation section explicitly says that detailed program-correctness and compilation lemmas are supporting results. The reference-NP section now describes both simulations. It would be wrong to call those results undisclosed assumptions or to reopen F04 simply because their machine-state proofs are not printed. [L, 1717–1733, 1879–1883.]

What is still needed is an early, exact statement of this boundary. “Machine-level steps” in the opening and “complete argument” in the reader promise leave a first reader to discover the import policy much later. A paragraph near the theorem should say which work is reconstructed and which results are used through contracts. A small table should then state the contracts: the concrete machine model and public encodings; callback correctness; polynomial charged cost and compilation; executable graph reduction; the finite-witness checker; and the two reference-NP simulations. [replacement_sections.md, §§1 and 5.]

One subtlety matters when making the implementation condition explicit. `ImplementedBy` equates the verifier with a **bounded unfolding** of its step function. The step function receives input, seed and answer history, and returns a verdict or the next address. At depth zero, another query request is converted into rejection. Thus this definition does not alone say that an unrestricted run of the raw callback voluntarily halts within the query budget. The present phrase “specified bounded query tree” is accurate. The proposed replacement preserves the cap rather than silently strengthening the assertion. [F/Complexity/CostModel.lean, `VerifierAlg`, `QueryTree.ofStep`, `Verifier.ImplementedBy`.]

With that boundary made explicit, the current title is defensible. A slightly more informative subtitle would be “Mathematical reconstruction and implementation contracts.” I would not retitle it “fully self-contained proof”: that would enlarge the promise beyond the chosen treatment of machines and simulations.

## 3. A concrete replacement architecture

### The first route: see the verifier, then discharge its contracts

Retain the precise theorem. Immediately afterward, state the model/import boundary and show the four primary polynomials, their four auxiliary line tables, and the seven checks. Explain the common-witness invariant before proving any local test: small rejection must supply four particular polynomials that continue to be used by every later check. Give the degree and label-domain information in the same place. Then prove completeness and outline the three strict soundness comparisons using named contracts whose proofs come later.

This is a legitimate forward-reference proof organization. The contracts remain theorems proved in the same document, and the main route explicitly points to their complete derivations. It is not a proposal to turn the central algebra into unexplained imports. A reader who wants to derive every lemma before using it can still follow the detailed chapters in their current order.

The early assembly should expose three obligations:

1. Each outer low-degree test returns an actual polynomial with the right degree and both required binary distances, for the same decoded object.
2. Difference consistency and the two corrected grid tests force the three polynomial constraints for those objects. The certificate reads are corrected because their targets are not uniformly distributed.
3. One parameter family satisfies all hypotheses; exact sampling, repetition, address bounds and machine contracts then establish the stated endpoint.

These are the reasons the later chapters exist. A general sentence saying “we combine the tests” cannot substitute for them. Conversely, the early assembly need not reproduce the determinant proof, Fourier calculation or every cost coefficient.

### The second route: enter and leave an individual lemma

Several current statements already do this well. The point-line theorem states concrete field and error bounds, and the local-correction section identifies a fixed spatial target and the averaged label. Preserve those statements. Add stable result names and a compact table of interfaces at the beginning of each technical chapter, rather than a new box after every paragraph.

An entry should tell a reader the input object, exact hypotheses, output object, probability space and downstream consumer. For example, the outer-correction entry must say: a fixed degree-bounded polynomial; homogeneous-label proximity; arbitrary auxiliary table; every spatial target; nonzero outer parameter; failure means rejection or a wrong returned bit; the bound averages the requested homogeneous label. It must point to the full assumptions, including `GoodNodes`, the generator-node condition where used, and the arbitrary-decoding degree bound. A table that says only “corrects a value” would erase the central work. [L, 1157–1204; F/Algebraic/BinaryLocalCorrectionSoundness.lean, `failure_le`.]

The return link should point to the actual use, not merely the next heading. B16 is used twice in B21; B25 is used for the vertex and edge constraints; the candidate-row crossing lemma is used in the modified ruled surface. These dependencies are more useful to the reference reader than a graph with only broad chapter names.

### What moves, what stays, what can be cut

This is a cut-and-move proposal, not an instruction to append all supplied replacement text. The present notation bridge at the final chapter is fixed and useful. A small translation row can accompany the early contracts: earlier digit radix `b` becomes `m`, field size `q` becomes `Q`, and final grid dimension is `d`. The specialist should remain a concise route through the same interfaces. The exposition should retain its worked examples. Neither companion should become a prerequisite for understanding a crucial hypothesis in the long account. The successful decision to place the label-domain example in the exposition, F16, is compatible with that principle: the long account already states and proves the necessary distinction.

## 4. Mathematical reconstruction: what held up under checking

### 4.1 Polynomial identities and the corrected weighted test

The opening algebra uses the right types of equality. Grid uniqueness is based on individual degree bounds; the distance estimate is based on total degree. The vanishing certificate comes from division by the coordinate vanishing polynomials, with the total-degree bookkeeping retained. The converse correctly allows an arbitrary certificate polynomial because the two substitutions agree on the grid. The exact-line argument invokes a sufficiently large field before promoting evaluation equalities to formal identities. The zero-dimensional and degree-zero cases are addressed rather than silently excluded. [L, A1–A5; algebra source scope in support/source_scope.md.]

The corrected short test needs a generator of the whole multiplicative group and the corresponding Lagrange weights. The propagation argument uses the nonzero final coefficient to determine the next value and then traverses the full orbit. Distinct nonzero interpolation nodes alone would not give that characterization. The robust weighted-constraint argument has a different, weaker node requirement: nonzero nodes make the affine sampling maps invertible; it does not require all weights to be nonzero or all nodes to be distinct. Keeping these two hypothesis sets separate is correct. [L, A6–A12; F/Algebraic/GeneratorLowDegreeTest.lean; WeightedConstraintSoundness.lean.]

The plurality argument is recoverable. Two weighted predictions are compared through a symmetric double sum; the fixed-center estimate charges `2nδ`, and the subsequent comparisons charge `nδ` and `2n²δ`. A failure budget strictly below one gives one successful configuration, forcing equality of deterministic endpoints. The smaller threshold later used by the generator test is justified by

\[
2(c+2)^2-\bigl(2(c+1)^2+3(c+1)\bigr)=c+3>0.
\]

The new Kaufman–Sudan specialization is a real specialization, not a decorative citation. Its distinguished form is `u`, the remaining forms are `u+a_i v`, and accepted value vectors form the weighted linear subspace. Their definition of two-ary independence requires the first form to be independent of each other form, which follows from `a_i≠0`; it does not require independence of every pair of forms. With `k=c+2`, their denominator becomes `(2k+1)(k−1)=2c²+7c+5`. No additional all-pairs assumption should be demanded. [L, paragraph after A10; KS, Definitions 2.3, 2.5, 2.8 and Theorem 2.9, original PDF p.5.]

The old-test counterexample remains correctly scoped. For `c=2`, characteristic-two fields of size `2^(2k)` admit the order-three nodes used in v1, and `x₁⁴` satisfies the old unit-weight test. A fibrewise root argument bounds its agreement with any degree-two polynomial by `4/q`. The arbitrarily large field family defeats the old local proximity claim; the finite example alone would not. It does not construct a cheating proof for the entire PCP verifier. My fresh GF(16) calculation gives zero old-test rejections out of 256 samples, 240 corrected-test rejections, and minimum quadratic distance `12/16`. [L, 323–344; V1 main.tex 716–742; checks/check_algebra.py and its output.]

### 4.2 Bivariate agreement, crossing and ruled-surface rigidity

The bivariate proof is one of the main reasons this account is useful. It deals with arbitrary approximating rows and columns and produces an individual-degree bound. The good-column count, polynomial matrix, nonzero minor, bounded polynomial kernel, clean rectangle, interpolation and final sharpening form an actual derivation. The proof does not replace an untracked rational kernel vector by a polynomial vector for free. The explicit cubic field bound is sufficient for the determinant and root-counting steps. [L, A15–A20; F/Algebraic/BivariateAgreement.lean and related regions; AS §5.2; ALMSS Theorem 69.]

A local ordering improvement is available without expanding the proof: state the clean-rectangle target immediately after choosing the good columns, then construct the error locator that produces it. Arora–Safra's passage is useful here because it presents the rectangle and interpolation consequence before the locator machinery. That order tells the reader what the polynomial kernel is supposed to buy. It does not make their formulation identical to the current arbitrary-approximant theorem.

The fixed-parameter line estimate correctly handles arbitrary ties among nearest polynomials. On lines inside the strict decoding radius the minimizer is unique, so transporting the line parameter transports the chosen polynomial. Exceptional lines are charged by the average-error bound. This yields the factor four at a fixed parameter without assuming a global coherent choice of minimizers. ALMSS's coherent-choice discussion does not eliminate the need for this extra argument in the formal variant. [L, A23–A25; F/Algebraic/PointLineParameterError.lean; ALMSS Remark 63.]

The mixed-plane repair is substantive. The current text defines the row and column polynomials and all four discrepancies, so the reader can distinguish own-line errors from transverse comparisons. Three means use a free parameter and are bounded by `δ′+1/q`; one comparison is at a fixed parameter and needs `4δ′+1/q`. The exceptional coordinates and averaging over `v,w,s` are accounted for. Reversing the local row/column comparison would invalidate this calculation, so these formulas must survive compression. [L, 594–638; F/Algebraic/MixedPlaneSampling.lean; PointLineEndpointConsistency.lean.]

The ruled-surface paragraph now explains the construction before the ledger: the zero column is the target corrected line, while nonzero columns have the sampled-line distribution. The modified row need not remain a nearest approximation, which is why the candidate-row crossing result has a fifth, own-row error. Most importantly, the polynomial `Q` is chosen before the target parameter `y₀`. A favorable random configuration may depend on `y₀`; the right-hand values it equates do not. Thus one fixed `Q` represents the whole target line. [L, 640–693; F/Algebraic/RuledSurfaceRigidity.lean.]

At the displayed extremal values, exact rational arithmetic gives

\[
\gamma=\frac1{625},\qquad
B=\frac{19217}{4{,}000{,}000},\qquad
\frac B\varepsilon+\gamma=\frac{19281}{40000}=0.482025<1.
\]

That verifies the numerical slack used in this step; it does not replace the preceding probability argument. The degree-zero case is separately valid, including characteristic two and zero-dimensional domains. [L, A28–A30; checks/check_algebra.json.]

### 4.3 Digit encoding and the two binary metrics

The injectivity repair now proves the right thing in the right order. The monomial calculation establishes

\[
\operatorname{Dec}(\operatorname{Enc}(p))=p\quad\text{in }F[Y]
\]

before injectivity is inferred. Equality of finite-field evaluations alone would not justify it: when `q<b^c`, `Y^q−Y` is a nonzero polynomial in the allowed formal degree range with zero evaluation function. The current text avoids both the earlier gap and the unnecessary remedy of imposing a degree-below-field-size restriction. It also keeps the honest degree `<b^c` distinct from the looser `≤cb^c` bound for an arbitrary decoded polynomial. [L, 800–833; F/Algebraic/SetMultilinear.lean; SetMultilinearDecode.lean.]

Full-label proximity cannot be substituted for homogeneous-label proximity. The latter label set can be very small inside the former. In the exposition's concrete three-coordinate example, there are 256 full cubic labels, 16 affine labels and 8 homogeneous labels. Replacing the homogeneous answers by another message changes four entries: full error `1/64`, homogeneous error `1/2`. I independently enumerated those counts. The long paper is justified in stating the distinction and leaving this optional worked example in the companion. [L, 869–871; exposition 797–808; F16; checks/binary_checks.json.]

The BLR/Fourier calculation, product-defect argument and threshold split produce one decoded value with both required metrics. The `r≥2` qualification now appears before the product argument; the `r=1` case uses additivity and unit preservation. A nonzero bilinear defect has support at least one quarter, and the charged product errors give the stated threshold. The arbitrary-row decoder does not assume all rows are good, does not introduce an efficient decoding algorithm, and does not choose unrelated witnesses for the two metrics. [L, B3–B11; F/Algebraic/HadamardSoundness.lean, BilinearHadamard.lean, HadamardRowDecoding.lean, BinaryLocalRowDecoding.lean; BCHKS §2.]

The corrected homogeneity attribution is also now accurate. Revised Claim 3.7 states the homogeneous conclusion for the scalar polynomial `P(Y)=ηY`. The current manuscript says that. The pinned Lean comment still describes a broader false clause absent from the two supplied editions. That comment is a separate historical-provenance issue: preserve the frozen source and annotate the mismatch if displaying it. A counterexample to the broad clause does not show that either retained paper printed the clause. F02 is fixed. [L, 886; V1 main.tex 829–853; V2 technical_tools.tex 168–198; F/Algebraic/HadamardComposition.lean 20–24.]

### 4.4 Correction, nonuniform requests and fresh randomness

The outer test and outer corrector intentionally use different parameter distributions. In the test, the line center is uniform, so `u+zv` is uniform even when `z=0`. In correction, the target `a` is fixed, so `z` must be nonzero for the direction map to make `a+zv` uniform. The manuscript keeps these distributions distinct. It also describes oriented pairs and allows the zero direction rather than claiming uniform sampling of nondegenerate geometric lines. [L, 1099–1204.]

The correction guarantee is exactly the useful one:

\[
\Pr[\text{reject or return a wrong bit}]
\le C(c)R_a+\delta+\frac{D}{2(q-1)}.
\]

It is unconditional, applies at every spatial target, and averages over the requested homogeneous label. It is not a guarantee for every fixed label and not correctness conditional on acceptance. In a small changed-case check, a table corrupted on one nonzero label at every point has small average label error but error one at that specified label. The qualifier therefore carries mathematical content. [L, B16–B18; F/Algebraic/BinaryLocalCorrectionSoundness.lean; checks/check_binary_contracts.py.]

The existing derivation already identifies the two inner-correction losses and the two rejection losses. The specialist explicitly collects them. I accept the decision not to add another cost table to the long account, F14. Requiring the exact format suggested by a previous reviewer would confuse an optional presentation choice with a proof obligation.

ZERO corrects both certificate requests because their target points can lie on exceptional slices where global average proximity says nothing. The paper preserves the common outer point and label and distinguishes the remaining independent call seeds of the formal variant. This gives a reason for the corrector, not just another named subroutine. [L, 1236–1276; F/Algebraic/BinaryZeroOnGridCorrection.lean; BinaryZeroOnGridSoundness.lean.]

For derived answers, the fresh mask is part of the actual probability space. One cannot choose a favorable mask after the fact and analyze a different fixed oracle. The point-dependent expression must separately be the evaluation of a bounded-degree formal polynomial. The actual-to-ideal rejection transfer is then inserted into ZERO, so the replacement error is multiplied by `1+2C(c)`. Both the polynomiality premise and that multiplier survive into final soundness. These are among the most important qualifications to preserve. [L, B23–B25, 1502–1517; F/Algebraic/HadamardDerivedOracle.lean; DerivedZeroOnGridSoundness.lean.]

### 4.5 One colouring from four fixed polynomials

The final proof chooses the four polynomials once and retains them. The colour and difference tables have the full degree-three distance needed for derived cubic reads; all four have the homogeneous distance needed for weighted comparisons and certificate correction. Difference consistency forces the formal identity `Q_*(X,Y)=P(X)−P(Y)`. The two grid constraints then use the same choices. The proof would fail if these existential witnesses were silently reselected between checks; the current text does not do that. [L, 1466–1522; F/Algebraic/ThreeColorVerifierSoundness.lean; ThreeColorDecodedSoundness.lean.]

The decoded constraints have degrees `3D` and `5D`, even though the four primary decoded polynomials have degree at most `D`. Using `5D` in the two ZERO arguments is correct. There is no need to redo the primary point-line decoding at degree `5D`: that work has already supplied the four witnesses. The `K₄` graph example also earns its place. Four field values in `F₄` can satisfy all the edge cube equations; the vertex condition is what restricts them to the three permitted colours. [L, 1359–1367, 1502–1517; F27, F33.]

The exact constants check out:

### 4.6 Parameters, fair bits, repetition and the NP bridge

The parameter family gets the asymptotics for the right reason. Choosing the least `a≥2` with `clog₂N≤a2^a` both accommodates the vertex set and, by failure at `a−1`, bounds the resources above by a logarithm. Merely finding a sufficiently large grid would not give that upper bound. The small-size convention, field inequality, honest-degree capacity, and natural-polynomial transport are retained. [L, 1550–1597, 1833–1861; F/Algebraic/ThreeColorResourceBounds.lean; F/Verifier/ReductionResources.lean.]

Replacing four nonzero-field draws by whole-field draws and accepting invalid seeds changes the rejection probability by the exact valid-seed fraction. The lower bound of one sixteenth is conservative and valid. Repetition samples independently against the same fixed proof. The elementary amplification calculation and the enormous explicit constant are consistent. No numerical optimization is necessary for the explanatory task. [L, 1600–1711; F/Algebraic/ThreeColorFullFieldSampling.lean; ThreeColorAmplifiedVerifier.lean.]

One locally incomplete explanation remains in the resource accounting: the exact seed polynomial is printed without enough branch accounting to derive every coefficient from the nearby prose. This is an explanation gap, not an asymptotic proof failure. I reconstructed the seven branches independently from the seed definitions. Their power-of-two exponents sum to

\[
35td+32tcm+69t+50+8\binom t2+6\binom t3;
\]

the four whole-field replacements add `4t`, giving the displayed `73t`. The replacement file supplies the complete table. Either include that table beside the formula or move both to an appendix and state the structural upper bound in the main route. [F/Algebraic/ThreeColorSeedSize.lean; replacement_sections.md §6; checks/check_final_arithmetic.py.]

The machine bridge has three distinct layers: the finite mathematical verifier, callback correctness and charged cost on arbitrary input words, and compilation into `FinTM2` with public encodings. The text does not equate a query bound with a time bound. The NP reduction also gives more than the phrase “by Cook–Levin”: bounded witness length, the pair delimiter, a common clock, finite machine alphabets and fixed local constraint arity are all used. Fixed arity is what makes the relation-to-clauses expansion polynomial. The OR-to-NAE and triangle gadgets passed fresh exhaustive finite checks. [L, 1714–1830; F/Complexity/ThreeColorBaseCorrect.lean, ThreeColorBasePolynomial.lean; F/Reduction/NPThreeColorRuntime.lean.]

Both reference-NP simulations are now explained at the requested level. In one direction an actual nondeterministic machine supplies a bounded choice word, and a deterministic path checker has its own polynomial runtime proof. Its runtime is not inferred merely from the witness bound. In the other, the concrete checker is translated to the reference pair format, malformed strings are guarded, and the bounded-guess machine computes and enforces the witness polynomial. It need not assume the original checker rejects overlong witnesses. These are concrete proved contracts, not supplied efficiency premises. [L, 1879–1883; F/Complexity/ReferenceNPBridge.lean, ReferencePairDecider.lean, ReferenceBoundedVerifier.lean, ReferenceNPConverse.lean.]

## 6. What to learn from the literature and the existing guide

The relevant writing comparisons are comparisons of mathematical jobs, not prose register scores. The revised ABSSW paper motivates its encoding by a concrete operation: the verifier needs one evaluation of a line polynomial without reading the entire high-degree object. Its later verifier section gives proof objects and checks before completeness and soundness. That order is a useful model for the proposed early assembly. It does not justify copying its asymptotic constants or its slightly different tester into this account. [V2 low_degree_test.tex, opening warmup; pcp-verifier.tex; ABSSW revised PDF §§4,7.]

Arora–Safra's clean-rectangle and interpolation sequence shows how to reveal the intended intermediate object before constructing an error locator. The long account can adopt that local order while retaining its stronger arbitrary-approximant contract and explicit constants. ALMSS supplies important historical theorem and crossing context, but its best-fitting-line formulation and tie convention should not be presented as exact substitutes for the formal statements. [AS §5.2; ALMSS Remark 63 and Theorem 69.]

Kaufman–Sudan is useful because it states a general testing contract whose hypotheses can actually be specialized. The newly added paragraph now performs that job. BCHKS is useful because its Fourier argument is compact and operational: rejection bias becomes a cubic Fourier moment, Parseval bounds that moment by the largest correlation, and the correlation supplies a nearby linear function. The current account preserves the calculation rather than replacing it by a vague invocation of linearity testing. [KS §2; BCHKS §2; L, 903–941.]

Troy's guide already states the endpoint, definitions, quantifier order, concrete models, canonical randomness and implementation relation clearly. Reproducing that interface guide at greater length is not the principal contribution here. The additional value lies in showing why the formal variant's hypotheses and constants compose into the theorem. The source owner's checking burden should therefore concentrate on the translations where mistakes are plausible: changed sampling domains, code-label restrictions, arbitrary versus honest decoded objects, bounded unfolding, and the simulation/import boundary. [G, reading-guide §§1–9 and the selected proof map.]

This also suggests a modest, reusable explanation method. For each difficult source result, record its exact mathematical contract, identify its consumer, read enough of the proof to recover the mechanism, and only then write the ordinary proof. Compare editions separately from comparing the formal statement. Attach source evidence with the right role: definition, ingredient, specialization, assembly or directly matching theorem. Finally test the route a reader actually takes. The digit-injectivity and homogeneity history demonstrate why all these steps matter: a valid evaluation theorem can be too weak for a prose inference, and a source comment can be unreliable about another edition's claim.
