# Review of the worked PCP exposition

15 November 2026 · Packet `pcp-review-round2-3-worked-exposition`

## Judgment

**Keep this paper and give it a more continuous mathematical spine. The repairs have substantially improved it, but it does not yet fully deliver the operational understanding promised to its stated reader.** Its strongest passages explain real obstacles and the mechanisms that overcome them. Its weakest transition asks the reader to assemble the actual verifier from several independently understandable examples, several kinds of addresses, and several imported robustness guarantees. That is the central remaining problem. Another round of isolated explanatory insertions will have diminishing returns.

The examined calculations and theorem interfaces support the construction. I found no counterexample to the assembled argument in the portions checked. I did find an incorrect explanation of the Boolean degree of a cubic field expression, together with several identifiable omissions in the operational account. The degree error does not invalidate the degree-three encoding or the reported resource bound. The teaching diagnosis is therefore more specific than either “the mathematics is wrong” or “the paper only needs polish.”

For the reader described in the brief—comfortable with polynomial arithmetic, finite sums and elementary probability, but without coding theory, complexity theory, Lean or graduate algebra—the appropriate goal is a **worked account of the verifier and of the architecture of its soundness proof**. The reader should be able to follow one colouring condition through its grid certificate to actual proof-bit addresses; explain the distributional obstacles; state the exact inputs supplied by the major testing theorems; and reconstruct why one decoded colouring satisfies all the graph conditions. The paper can accomplish that while importing the deeper testing results explicitly. It need not reproduce their entire proofs or the machine-level implementation.

The current paper already achieves much of the local explanation needed for that goal. It does not yet complete the promised continuous example. This return supplies one, with independently checked arithmetic, rather than merely recommending that an example be added. It also supplies a conservative textual patch, a complete audit of the 44 original findings, and a paragraph map. None has been merged into the authoritative paper.

Throughout this review, **[M]** denotes the frozen `papers/expository/main.md`, with the line numbers in the supplied packet. **[F]** denotes the supplied Lean sources under `formal/lean/PCP/`, identified by filename and declaration. **[A2]** denotes the actual enclosed ABSSW version-two TeX, and **[AB]** the enclosed January 8, 2007 Arora–Barak book. The source pins, hashes, reading scope and commands are in [coverage.md](coverage.md). Source inspection, finite execution and teaching judgment are distinguished there. This is an AI review with mathematical background, not an independent human learning study or a new Lean build.

## 1. What the paper should teach

The opening correctly distinguishes a constant number of certificate-bit reads from constant computation, fixes the certificate before randomness, and explains polynomial bounds before using them as promises. The theorem is quantified over a single verifier and fixed constants for each language. These are useful repairs. The diluted K4 example then gives the reader a concrete reason that checking a few ordinary constraints cannot establish the desired theorem. This opening should survive. [M, lines 8–146; repair audit PA-01–PA-03.]

The stronger promise at lines 39–40 is that the reader will command the mechanism from colouring constraints to bits. That promise requires continuity between representations. A learner who understands each isolated calculation can still be unable to say which table is being queried, which polynomial is merely a witness in the analysis, and which apparently single query expands into two masked reads. Those distinctions determine both the algorithm and its soundness; they cannot be delegated entirely to the reader's reconstruction.

At present the examples move from F5 root counting and interpolation, to F4 colours, to a separate F5 grid certificate, to a different F5 digit polynomial, to a three-bit Hadamard word, to an F4 cubic label, and finally to the general eight-table construction. Each example has a reason to exist. Their succession nevertheless stops short of carrying one object through the full chain. Section 8 is where the unpaid explanatory work accumulates: representations, oracle types, low-degree testing, binary encoding, weighted correction, same-witness guarantees and target-specific rejection all arrive close together. [M, §§3–8.]

The remedy is to retain the examples that demonstrate genuinely different phenomena and add a continuing example that joins the main operations. Do not force every calculation into F4. The corrected F5 quartic is valuable precisely because its formal degree distinction survives as a function on that field. The 256/16/8 label-space example is valuable because it exhibits a change of measure. The small continuing example has a different job: show how the represented object persists while the addresses and queries change.

After the revision, a reader should be able to do four things without consulting a Lean file:

1. Describe the stored primary and auxiliary tables and execute the data reads of a specified honest ZERO branch.
2. Explain why proximity on the ambient domain does not justify a direct read at a prescribed point, a particular auxiliary anchor, or a special derived label.
3. State which theorem produces one common decoded polynomial and which theorem controls a rejecting corrector at a fixed target.
4. Reconstruct the coupled inequality that turns rare actual rejection into exact polynomial identities, then into a proper colouring.

Those are observable mathematical capabilities. They are more useful acceptance criteria than sentence-length statistics, a count of repaired comments, or whether an expert can paraphrase the theorem. They also establish a manageable boundary: the deep low-degree and generalized Hadamard arguments may remain explicit inputs, with routes to their proofs.

## 2. Preserve the arguments that already do their jobs

Sections 1–5 have a strong causal sequence. A sparse violation defeats naive sampling. Polynomial distance offers a way to spread disagreement, while the arbitrary-table warning blocks the illegitimate assumption that a received word already is a polynomial. Interpolation constructs the honest object. The colour equations distinguish valid colours from proper edges. The assignment of four field values to K4 shows why the vertex condition cannot be omitted. Grid vanishing is distinguished from ambient vanishing, and the two certificate identities are both justified. The thin-slice example then explains why ambient proximity alone cannot protect prescribed queries. These passages carry substantive teaching work. [M, lines 48–414.]

The corrected digit example is also worth keeping. The polynomial is now quartic over F5, rather than an example whose displayed high degree collapses as a function through the identity Y^5=Y. The supplied check confirms that no polynomial of degree at most three has the same five evaluations. More importantly, the text retains the distinction between an honest set-multilinear encoding and an arbitrary polynomial produced by a low-degree test. Removing that distinction would make the subsequent degree bound false. [M, §6; PA-13, PA-14; `examples/check_exposition_examples.py`.]

Section 7's progression from an actual Hadamard word to linearity testing, chosen-observation correction and nonlinear labels is productive. The false product assignment shows that linearity in the label coefficients does not force evaluation at one message. The mask argument fixes the requested point and label before drawing the mask, which is exactly the needed distributional statement. The full/affine/homogeneous example in Section 8 then demonstrates why restricting an error bound to a small label subset is an additional mathematical demand. [M, lines 559–830.]

The soundness coupling in Section 9 should be the climax of the main route. It keeps the correction rejection terms, compares experiments with the same seeds, and derives an event containment rather than multiplying unjustified success probabilities. The text also distinguishes the decoded polynomials used in the argument from objects reconstructed by the verifier. Preserve that distinction and the actual inequality. A smoother narrative that deletes these qualifications would be a worse mathematical explanation. [M, lines 964–1056; F, `BinaryZeroOnGridSoundness.lean`.]

Finally, preserve the version and credit boundaries. The old order-three/unit-weight test is genuinely defective; the corrected generator test is a different test. A finite F16 calculation illustrates the defect, while the family over F_(2^(2k)) shows that increasing the field size does not rescue the old characterization. The exposition correctly attributes the earlier correction and distinguishes the source paper's query count from the formal variant's. This is useful evidence about the construction, not a new priority claim by the exposition or this review. [M, §12; A2, `technical_tools.tex`; F, `HadamardTest.lean` and related soundness sources.]

## 4. New findings and their consequences

### N1. Complete one colouring-to-bits chain

**Consequence: central to the advertised teaching goal.** The missing continuous example is a larger issue than any single notation error. It leaves the reader to infer how the polynomial residual, its certificate, the line encoding and the bit labels belong to one check. The replacement sample in [replacement_trace.md](replacement_trace.md) supplies this chain.

Take H={0,1} in F4, colour the two endpoints of one edge by 1 and omega, and use

\[
\chi(X)=1+\omega^2X.
\]

Its vertex residual is

\[
P(X)=\chi(X)^3-1
=(X^2+X)(X+\omega^2),
\]

so the actual colouring supplies the certificate

\[
M(X,U)=U(X+\omega^2),\qquad Z_H(X)=X^2+X.
\]

At the off-grid point x=omega, P(x)=1, M(x,0)=0 and M(x,Z_H(x))=1. That is an instructive choice: the colour polynomial is zero there, yet there is no invalid vertex because omega is not a vertex label. The two ambient identities still hold.

The sample then follows one possible sampled homogeneous observation, its fresh mask, the two certificate lines, the digit substitution, the interpolation weights and their pulled-back bit labels. It lists all eight auxiliary data reads, both primary comparison reads, and the two masked colour reads. The returned certificate bits are 0 and 1, and ZERO checks exactly the identities just calculated.

This is an honest branch trace with deliberately small parameters. It leaves the syntactic row tests at their honest-completeness guarantee and does not pretend to run the complete 187-query verifier or instantiate its large-field soundness regime. Equal displayed values of independently sampled seeds are identified as possible joint outcomes, not permission to share those random variables. The derivation and script were checked by a second mathematical implementation.

The example does not replace the general multivariate quotient sum. Keep the explanation of M(X,U)=sum_j U_j Q_j and its degree bound. The existing two-variable F5 example can support that construction. It also should not be stretched into a tiny-field demonstration of every edge residual: in this graph the resulting edge expression has a finite-field function coincidence that would distract from the purpose. One continuous vertex condition is enough to make the operations visible; the general soundness argument handles both constraints.

### N2. Make the addresses and ZERO interface unambiguous

**Consequence: blocks reconstruction of the algorithm; overlaps the remaining part of PA-20.** Define the power-point map where it first arises:

\[
\Phi:F\longrightarrow F^{\{0,\ldots,c-1\}\times\{0,\ldots,b-1\}},
\qquad \Phi(z)_{i,j}=z^{j b^i}.
\]

The coordinates with j=0 are one. Section 6 already explains this substitution, but Section 8 starts using its unnamed map at lines 752 and 762. Naming it beside the first worked tuple removes avoidable guesswork. [M, §§6 and 8.]

Next type an auxiliary address as an oriented outer line, an inner point and a binary label. For an outer table on F^k, these components are (a,v) in F^k times F^k, w in F^(cb), and an **affine** Boolean label R. Auxiliary labels are affine even when the corresponding primary colour table has degree-three labels. Weighted labels are particular homogeneous members of that affine domain. The current four-pair inventory is useful, but it needs these types to become executable.

Finally explain that p(x,L) in the generic ZERO interface is a bit oracle for the proposed residual. It is not an extra stored primary table. In the assembled vertex check it is implemented by two masked queries into chi; in the edge check it is implemented through chi-prime with the publicly computable adjacency factor incorporated in the requested label. This explains both why the stored inventory remains four primary/auxiliary pairs and why a nominal single generic query expands into two actual reads. [M, lines 881–885 and §9; F, `HadamardDerivedOracle.lean`.]

### N3. Explain why the anchored auxiliary row is tested again

**Consequence: hides the reason for a major algorithmic step and for the C R_a term.** The earlier low-degree test controls an average over oriented line indices (a,v). A corrector at a prescribed target a_* only samples the fibre (a_*,v). All rows in that fibre can be bad while their global proportion is only Q^(-k). Randomising the direction does not randomise the anchor.

For Q=4 and k=2 there are 256 oriented indices. Corrupting the 16 rows at one anchor gives global fraction 1/16 and conditional fraction 1 at that target. This does not construct a cheating proof that passes: the row test or comparison may reject it. It explains precisely why global proximity alone cannot justify the correction and why the actual target-dependent rejection rate belongs in the bound.

ABSSW v2 states this obstruction before Algorithm 5.1. Restore it before the exposition's four-step corrector. The reader then sees three different concentrations of error and their corresponding repairs:

| Where errors can concentrate | What the construction uses |
| --- | --- |
| A special primary target slice | Random-line correction at the prescribed point |
| All auxiliary rows at one fixed anchor | Retesting and the actual rejection term R_a |
| A special subset of nonlinear labels | A fresh uniform mask after fixing the request |

These explanations do different jobs. Combining them into a general assurance that redundancy corrects errors would lose the mechanism. [M, lines 391–414, 711–725 and 832–872; A2, `local_correction.tex`, “Expected Oracles and Queries”; [changed-case derivations](checks/changed_cases/DERIVATIONS.md).]

### N4. Correct the reason that the cubic observation is quadratic

**Consequence: a real false explanation, with a small local repair.** Lines 688–690 attribute the quadratic label in F4 to that field being tiny. In every characteristic-two extension, squaring is linear over F2. Field multiplication is bilinear in binary coordinates. Consequently u^3=u·u^2 has Boolean coordinate degree at most two, and a linear observation of u^3-1 has the same bound.

The degree-three code remains sufficient and is the code used by the pinned formal verifier. Retain it and its resource accounting. An improved degree estimate alone does not define or verify an optimized protocol. Suggested wording:

> Squaring is linear in binary coordinates in every characteristic-two field, so L(u^3-1) has Boolean degree at most two. We retain the degree-three encoding used by the source construction; the reported query bounds refer to that verifier.

The shorter alternative is simply to say that the computed label belongs to the chosen degree-three space. Either repair distinguishes field-polynomial degree, Boolean degree and the code's chosen degree bound. [M, lines 647–709; [mathematical audit](mathematical_audit.md), §1.]

### N5. State the imported contracts at the point of use

**Consequence: the reader can otherwise confuse three different forms of robustness.** The exposition should not add full proofs of all imported results. It should expose their inputs and outputs consistently. A useful compact organization is:

| Available information | Additional theorem or operation | What it gives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small point/line comparison rejection on arbitrary tables | Point-versus-line soundness | One ambient low-degree polynomial |
| An inner row close to a fixed-degree polynomial | Weighted interpolation along a random direction | A chosen evaluation with error bounded by that distance |
| Small binary syntactic-test rejection | Generalized Hadamard and encoded-line soundness | One common witness with the required label-domain bounds |
| Homogeneous proximity of the primary table, arbitrary anchored auxiliary rows | Retest, compare away from the target, return or reject | A fixed-target failure bound containing actual rejection |
| Rare rejection by the received-data ZERO experiment | Coupling and polynomial distance | Exact identities for the same decoded witnesses |

The generalized Hadamard row test deserves one explicit box. It includes unit, affine-consistency and corrected product checks; the preceding BLR illustration and one displayed product identity do not specify the full test. The inspected theorem gives, for rejection at most delta<1/28, one message with full-label error at most delta and affine-restriction error at most 3 delta. State its exact domain and degree assumptions beside it. [F, `HadamardSoundness.test_soundness`, lines 28–61.]

Preserve the actual randomization if adding pseudocode. The inner binary low-degree test is a fair-coin mixture between a row test and a weighted interpolation test. It does not run both on every seed. The outer encoded-line test combines a primary row test, that inner test, and a corrected comparison. An illustrative stronger procedure could be reasonable, but it would be a variant and could not inherit the pinned count automatically. [F, `BinaryLowDegreeTest.lean`, lines 25–55; `EncodedLineLowDegreeTest.lean`, lines 52–57.]

## 5. Mathematical findings that constrain the redesign

The new narrative must preserve several distinctions that the current text handles correctly. The supporting [mathematical audit](mathematical_audit.md) gives declaration-level evidence; the following are the consequences for the author.

### Honest structure is stronger than the tested property

The honest digit encoding is set-multilinear, but the nearby polynomial supplied by an inner low-degree theorem need not be. A changed example makes the difference exact. Take b=3 and c=2 and let the arbitrary inner polynomial be

\[
Q(W)=W_{1,2}^{\,2}.
\]

Its inner degree is two. Under Phi it becomes Y^12, exceeding the honest univariate capacity of degree below nine. It attains the sharper substitution bound c(b-1)b^(c-1)=12 and satisfies the convenient bound cb^c=18. On F16 no polynomial of degree below nine has the same evaluations: its difference with Y^12 would have degree twelve and sixteen roots. This is a root-bound deduction, not an exhaustive search through coefficient lists. The finite substitution was checked separately. [M, §6; [changed cases](checks/changed_cases/DERIVATIONS.md), §2.]

Thus the paper is right to enlarge the decoded degree. A proposed simplification that treats a tested received row as an honest encoding would break the argument. The useful lesson is broader than this example: a property test supplies its stated property, not every structural feature of the honest prover's construction.

### The same witness must satisfy both distance statements

The combined low-degree theorem supplies one polynomial P with both the full degree-r-label and homogeneous-label distance bounds. Those are not two unrelated existential statements. The nonlinear residual tests and the homogeneous difference/correction tests have to constrain the same decoded colouring. Separately choosing witnesses would leave no single colouring known to satisfy all the identities. The current text and source preserve this dependency. [M, lines 783–830 and §9; F, `EncodedLineLowDegreeTest.exists_close`, `EncodedLineLowDegreeSoundness.lean`, lines 321–359.]

Likewise, full-label proximity does not automatically imply comparable proximity on a thin subset. Changing four homogeneous positions in a 256-position full row gives full error 1/64 but homogeneous error 1/2. The example is doing essential measure-theoretic work at an elementary level. Keep the explicit domains, especially the distinction between homogeneous and affine labels.

### A mask must be fresh conditionally, not merely uniform overall

The current mask proof is correct. To test understanding, change the experiment. Let the label space have 256 elements, let h be the honest evaluation word, and corrupt only g(0), giving error 1/256. Draw U uniformly and set both the requested label and the mask to U. Both are individually uniform, but the translated query is always zero. The corrected answer is wrong for every U except zero, hence with probability 255/256.

For a fixed nonzero request with a genuinely fresh uniform mask, exactly two masks cause an error, giving 2/256. The two reads need not be independent; they need the correct individual distributions conditional on the fixed request. This is a successful transfer of the paper's argument, not a counterexample to it. It is an excellent exercise because the words “uniform mask” alone no longer suffice. [M, lines 711–725; F, `HadamardDerivedOracle.answer_failure_le`, `labels_failure_le` and `averaged_failure_le`; [changed cases](checks/changed_cases/DERIVATIONS.md), §1.]

### Fixed target, random observation, rejection included

The outer corrector's theorem is target-specific, but it is not a guarantee for every fixed label. The ambient point a is prescribed and the homogeneous label is sampled uniformly. The failure event includes both rejection and a wrong returned bit. In the inspected notation its bound has the form

\[
\Pr[\text{reject or return the wrong bit at }a]
\le C R_a+\delta+\frac{D}{2(Q-1)}.
\]

The primary table is close to the same P on homogeneous labels; P and the decoded line polynomials have the stipulated degree bounds; the interpolation nodes and field-size hypotheses hold. No premise assumes the auxiliary rows are honest or R_a is already small. The retest and nonzero comparison make the statement useful even for arbitrary anchored data. [F, `BinaryLocalCorrectionSoundness.failure_le`, lines 222–313.]

ZERO uses a common random homogeneous label for the two calls and the appropriate independent remaining seeds. Each marginal call has the distribution required by the theorem. If either call rejects, ZERO rejects. Therefore the average target-dependent rejection cost is controlled by the enclosing test's rejection probability. The proof does not require independent failure events or uniformly small failure at every target. Those are exactly the quantifiers that a streamlined explanation must retain. [F, `BinaryZeroOnGridSoundness.twoCallCheck` and `two_corrector_rejection_bound`, lines 93–135.]

### Why the coupled inequality works

For the generic ZERO check, the inspected source gives

\[
\frac{1-Dh/Q}{2}
\le (1+2C)r+\delta_P+2\delta_M+\frac{D}{Q-1}
\]

when a required identity is false. The left side comes from a nonzero polynomial being nonzero at many ambient points, followed by a random homogeneous observation detecting a nonzero field value with probability one half. The right side accounts for actual rejection, primary-answer error and both rejecting correctors. [F, `BinaryZeroOnGridSoundness.rejectionRate_lower_bound`, lines 249–267.]

In the assembled construction the derived residual answer is first replaced by the ideal answer at cost at most 2 epsilon. The certificate retains homogeneous error epsilon, and 5D is a safe common degree bound. This yields the exposition's inequality

\[
\frac{1-5Dh/Q}{2}
\le (1+2C)(\kappa+2\varepsilon)
+2\varepsilon+\frac{5D}{Q-1}.
\]

The event relation is simple and powerful: if the ideal test rejects but the received-data test accepts on the same seeds, some supplied answer changed the decision. A union bound controls that possibility. There is no need to assert that the different errors are independent. Under the displayed parameter conditions, the ideal lower bound is at least 3/8 while the remaining upper bound is strictly smaller. This forces the identities. The colour and edge residual bounds 3D and 5D, the substitution bound Dh, and the common bound 5Dh are consistent with the inspected source. [M, lines 964–1056; F, `ThreeColorSoundnessBudget.lean` and `ThreeColorVerifierSoundness.lean`.]

Keep the toy budget exercise's strictness. At the proposed weaker boundary, the upper and lower bounds can both be 3/8, so no contradiction follows. Similarly, superpolynomial seed enumeration invalidates that proof of the reverse inclusion; it does not prove that the language lies outside NP. These are correct retained caveats, not qualifications to edit away. [M, §11; KEEP findings in the audit.]

### Resource claims refer to one particular variant

The seven branch bounds sum to 20c+107, or 187 at c=4. The fair-bit wrapper retains the exact rejection factor ((Q-1)/Q)^4, which is at least 1/16. The supplied arithmetic gives the one-run gap 1/1137847282503168. These statements refer to the pinned formal variant, including its degree-three row code and its testing procedure. The source paper's expanded appendix is useful for presentation, but it is not an interchangeable implementation from which to copy a total count. [F, `ThreeColorVerifier.lean`, `ThreeColorFullFieldSampling.rejectionRate_eq`, `ThreeColorBinaryVerifier.gap`; supplied finite-check stdout.]

The parameter analysis also separates three issues correctly: how many proof addresses exist, how many random bits are sampled, and how much time the algorithm uses. Bounding address length alone is not a runtime proof. The theorem's bound covers addresses on arbitrary oracle answers, and the reverse inclusion uses a computable randomness budget and a finite proof prefix. These details matter to the actual PCP statement even if the exact constants move to an appendix. The runtime bridge was inspected at its program-bearing interface, not re-established by this review. [M, §§2 and 10; F, `PCP.np_subset_pcp`, `PCP.pcp_subset_np_concrete`, `NPThreeColorRuntime.of_inNP_program`.]

## 6. What the literature suggests changing

The useful literature comparison concerns how a source teaches the same operation, not whether its prose has similar sentence lengths. The exact enclosed versions governed these comparisons.

**Arora–Barak, §§18.4.1–18.4.2.** The book defines the code, explains the limitation of exact testing from a few samples, states a proximity guarantee, and presents a chosen-value decoder as an executable procedure. It then keeps a particular represented object visible while describing what an honest proof contains and what the verifier does. The current Section 7 already has much of the first virtue. Extend that input–queries–output pattern to the outer corrector, and keep one colouring residual visible through the nested encodings. Importing the book's different QUADEQ construction would add work without solving this exposition's problem. [AB, PDF pp. 379–382, printed pp. 18.13–18.16.]

**Arora–Barak, §19.3.2.** The deeper linearity proof appears with the Fourier framework it needs. This supports a sensible boundary for the current paper: teach the useful operation and state the sharp theorem, with its proof on another route. Requiring a Fourier primer in the main explanation would undermine the stated prerequisites. [AB, PDF pp. 406–407, printed pp. 19.6–19.7.]

**ABSSW v2, §§3–5.** The source emphasizes that a long line record is used to obtain one evaluation, not to recover every coefficient. Its warmup distinguishes an arbitrary dishonest row from a low-degree row and distinguishes a power-point Phi(lambda) from a uniform ambient point. Its correction section explicitly explains the fixed-anchor fibre problem. Those are the causal explanations to restore around the exposition's compact algorithm; the complete technical proofs need not be copied. [A2, `technical_tools.tex`, `low_degree_test.tex`, `local_correction.tex`.]

**ABSSW v2, Appendix A.** The explicit verifier and honest prover are useful models for making both sides of the protocol visible. Its warning about redundant all-cubic encodings also demonstrates why the formal variant must be tracked carefully. The new trace in this return uses the exposition's colour/certificate label distinction and avoids claiming a total protocol count. [A2, `appendix-full-verifier.tex`, lines 1–246.]

**BLR, retained author version, §4.2.** The original group-valued self-testing and corrected-homomorphism argument is valuable proof and historical context. Its assumptions and notation are heavier than the current elementary binary example, so it is not the best prose template for this reader. Keep the distinction between the original test and the sharper bound credited to BCHKS. This review does not pretend that a teaching comparison with BLR independently verifies every theorem in the later literature. [BLR, retained PDF pp. 15–19; exact coverage in `evidence/teaching_coverage.md`.]

**Lee's original reading guide.** That guide already explains what the Lean theorem says, how definitions correspond, how canonical randomness works, what the large constants are, and where the low-degree variants differ. The new exposition's distinctive contribution should be the mechanism: why the objects exist and how the verifier uses them. Reprinting more metadata would not supply that contribution. The teaching reviewer read the full enclosed guide; the mathematical review checked selected corresponding declarations.

External research confirmed that the official ECCC record lists the corrected revision on 22 October 2026 and explicitly records the Lemma 3.3 correction and associated test changes. The local version-two source remains the authority for the technical comparison; live metadata is not evidence of a new Lean replay. See [ECCC TR26-134](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/134/). Direct live retrieval of some other primary locations was blocked, so the coverage record identifies the retained originals actually used instead of suggesting that every web fetch succeeded.

## 7. A concrete replacement opening and route

The following opening would make the reader's task and the imported-proof boundary more explicit. It is proposed text, not an assertion that the current introduction contains no useful material:

> A graph comes with a claim: its vertices can be coloured with three colours so that every edge joins different colours. An ordinary certificate lists the colours. Checking a few randomly chosen edges can miss a single bad edge, even when the graph has no proper colouring.
>
> The PCP theorem permits a different certificate. The prover fixes a redundant bit string before the checker chooses its random bits. A proper colouring has an encoding that always passes. If no proper colouring exists, every proposed encoding is rejected with a fixed positive probability; independent repetition makes that probability at least one half. The number of certificate bits read is bounded independently of the graph's size. The checker still reads the graph and performs polynomial computation.
>
> We will follow one colouring condition into its polynomial certificate and then into the bit addresses used to check it. Along the way we will see why low-degree testing, extra variables for line polynomials, binary observation labels and correction at prescribed points are needed. The small examples explain these operations. The general soundness argument uses stated testing and correction theorems, whose proofs are supplied in the full account. The final task is to show why the same decoded colouring must satisfy both the vertex and edge conditions.
>
> The construction is due to Amireddy, Behera, Srinivasan, Sudan and Willumsgaard; the formal version discussed here is Troy Lee's pinned development. We use the corrected October 2026 paper and identify consequential differences in the formal version where they matter.
