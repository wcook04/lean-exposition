# Specialist PCP paper: second review and proposed revision

**Reviewed edition:** lean-exposition `f992ebd7fb9f16f921ec05c4c3f93f33ad933ad6`; reader `f500810cd5de90b100940ea7d7d048f54f3bf7c7`; Lean source `a0ce13ed0fa264f051c1278e039596ce65557fa4`. **Date:** 15 November 2026. This is an AI-assisted mathematical, editorial and source-fidelity review. The proposed manuscript is a separate, unmerged edit candidate.

## 1. Judgment: keep this paper, but let its actual contribution organize it

The paper now succeeds at a substantial part of its intended job. In particular, its central correction proof is locally reconstructible. Section 5 accounts for the use of nonuniform certificate targets, the arbitrary auxiliary tables, the random homogeneous label, the root-bound loss, and the dependence on the corrector's actual rejection. Section 6 then chooses four decoded polynomials once and proves the colouring conditions for those same choices. Those are meaningful improvements over the preceding edition. I found no false central theorem, invalid central soundness implication, or incorrect displayed error coefficient in the regions checked against the source.

The next revision should improve how the reader reaches that argument. The current paper gives several lower-level testing proofs substantial attention before presenting the received proof's complete table inventory. A reader encounters a weighted characterization, its robust form, the historical defective test, Hadamard evaluation structure, a restricted-label estimate, inner decoding, outer testing, and a point-line supplier before seeing the four primary tables together on PDF page 9. The paper has the right mathematics, but its order still makes the reader assemble the construction retrospectively.

The defensible purpose is: **explain how low observed rejection of arbitrary binary tables yields a colouring through one fixed tuple of decoded polynomials, while keeping the implementation's distributions and resources accurate.** This is useful work beyond the theorem-and-definitions route in Lee's guide. A specialist who has not read this construction should leave able to explain why the two encodings occur in this order, what a physical bit query reads, why special certificate points require correction, and how the same witnesses survive all seven checks. The paper need not also teach every supplier proof in miniature.

I recommend retaining the specialist format and revising its architecture. I do not recommend replacing it with a general PCP survey, a shorter publicity summary, or a second copy of the long account. The proposed manuscript in this return makes the governing inference explicit on the first page, moves the eight-table inventory immediately after the certificate construction, types the auxiliary row, distinguishes the three label domains in one place, and states the supplier boundaries more clearly. It preserves the detailed correction and common-decoding arguments. It is an actual edited source file with a diff, not an outline of work left to do.

This judgment separates fidelity from usefulness. A formally defensible paragraph can still arrive too early, carry too many jobs, or leave the reader unsure which object a theorem concerns. Conversely, an unperformed paragraph split does not erase a successful mathematical repair. The audit below does not treat the number of green rows as a quality score.

## 3. The mathematical core is now doing real explanatory work

### 3.1 Honest encodings and decoded objects have different guarantees

The digit map starts from a line polynomial of degree strictly below $b^c$. Its honest encoding is set-multilinear, has total degree at most $c$, and evaluates back to the original line under the substitution $\Phi$. Soundness is deliberately weaker: a received row can decode to any polynomial $U$ of total degree at most $c$. It need not have the honest monomial pattern. Nevertheless $U\circ\Phi$ has degree at most $D=cb^c$, and the later field-size and root bounds use that inflated degree.

Current equations (3)–(5) make this distinction correctly. `SetMultilinearDecode.lean` supports the arbitrary-$U$ conclusion. Preserve it. Replacing this by “the row decodes to the line it encodes” would quietly assume the property that the test is supposed to establish.

The certificate degree calculation is also now local. For a nonzero quotient, $\deg U_i\le\deg A-h$; multiplication by the separate variable $Y_i$ keeps its degree at most $\deg A$ because $h\ge1$. The converse does not require the proposed certificate to be linear in $Y$: on the grid, $Z_H(x)=0$, so its two second arguments coincide. This is a particularly efficient explanation and should survive unchanged. [Current paper, §§2–3; `GridVanishing.lean`; `SetMultilinearDecode.lean`.]

### 3.2 Full, affine and homogeneous label domains are not interchangeable

The repaired row argument supplies one decoded field value with both required distances. For a good row of rejection $\epsilon$, full-label error is at most $\epsilon$. Affine consistency contributes its own check failure plus the two-query correction loss, giving at most $3\epsilon$ on affine labels. Homogeneous labels occupy half that affine domain, so the same value has homogeneous error at most $6\epsilon$. Bad rows are absorbed into the common $28\epsilon$ bound using the threshold $1/28$.

The important information is both the domain and the common witness. Full proximity cannot simply be restricted to the homogeneous subspace. In the finite diagnostic included here, with four binary coordinates and degree-three labels, there are $2^{15}=32,768$ full labels and only $16$ homogeneous labels. Flipping precisely those 16 positions gives

$$
\Delta_{\rm full}=\frac1{2048},\qquad
\Delta_{\rm hom}=1.
$$

That diagnostic also appeared in the previous review; it has been rerun here, not claimed as a new discovery. It is a counterexample to an invalid inference about distances, not a cheating proof against the actual combined tester. The affine-consistency test sees the corruption at constant rate.

The analogous inner-polynomial statement is unconditional and again gives one $U$ for both bounds. Each of the two coin-selected branches rejects with probability at most $2\kappa$. Row decoding, the weighted test and the binary-to-field factor give the printed constant

$$
K=4(c+2)^2\bigl(112(c+2)+60\bigr).
$$

The trivial distance bound handles the regime in which the weighted theorem's small-rejection hypothesis is unavailable. Do not turn this into a small-error-only decoding theorem; the local corrector needs to use it on arbitrary auxiliary rows. [Current §4; `HadamardSoundness.lean`; `HadamardRowDecoding.lean`; `BinaryLocalRowDecoding.lean`.]

### 3.3 The three outer checks now share the right row

The outer test is conjunctive. Its Hadamard check is on the primary row at $u$. Its inner test and comparison both concern the auxiliary row indexed by the same oriented line $(u,v)$. The comparison takes place at $u+zv$, with $u,v,z$ uniform over their full domains, including $z=0$.

That distribution is sound because the starting point $u$ is itself uniform. The same statement will not be true when the starting point is a prescribed target; that difference is exactly why the corrector later uses $z\ne0$.

After choosing the primary field function and decoding the auxiliary rows, the three discrepancy costs are $(c+1)K\kappa$, $\kappa$, and $28\kappa$. Averaging a homogeneous label halves field disagreement, so

$$
J=2\bigl((c+1)K+29\bigr)
$$

bounds the field point-line error by $J\kappa$. The point-line supplier gives field distance at most $2J\kappa$; encoding halves that contribution, and adding the primary error gives $S\kappa$ with $S=28+J$. Current equation (13) concerns one polynomial, on both domains, under its displayed smallness condition.

The source permits arbitrary oriented line rows, including zero directions; it does not assume global agreement among different parametrizations. The implementation's whole-field sampling and explicit constants must not be silently replaced by the corresponding preprint variant. [Current §4; `EncodedLineLowDegreeTest.lean`; `EncodedLineLowDegreeSoundness.lean`; `PointLineSoundness.lean`.]

### 3.4 The fixed-target corrector is the paper's strongest explanation

Fix a spatial target $a$. The primary table is close to a polynomial $P$ on average, but that says nothing useful about its values on a specially chosen small subset. The corrector therefore samples a random direction, tests the associated auxiliary row, compares a corrected point on that line with a primary observation, and only then returns the corrected observation at the origin. Its output is one bit or rejection.

Let $R_a$ be its actual rejection probability. Since failure of the inner test forces rejection, unconditional row decoding gives mean auxiliary decoding error at most $KR_a$. Set $e=(c+1)KR_a$. Each of the two corrected auxiliary observations can be replaced by the observation of the chosen line polynomial at cost at most $e$.

The two corrections share their inner direction and homogeneous label. This does not invalidate the estimate: each has the necessary marginal error bound, and the proof uses a union bound. It does not multiply their error probabilities or assume that the two failures are independent.

If the decoded line polynomial $T_v$ has the wrong value at zero, then $T_v(Y)-P(a+Yv)$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $D$. It has at most $D$ roots among the $q-1$ permitted nonzero parameters. Averaging the homogeneous label gives the crucial inequality

$$
\Pr_{v,L}[L(T_v(0))\ne L(P(a))]
\le
\Pr_{v,z,L}[L(T_v(z))\ne L(P(a+zv))]
+\frac{D}{2(q-1)}.
$$

This is an averaged-label statement. It does not say that every fixed label detecting the error at the origin must also detect it at the sampled point.

At the sampled point, replacement of the auxiliary observation costs $e$, the failed comparison costs $R_a$, and primary replacement costs $\delta$. The last term is justified because $a+zv$ is uniform for every nonzero $z$. At the origin there is a second replacement cost $e$. The failure event includes explicit rejection, adding another $R_a$. Hence

$$
\Pr[\text{reject or return a wrong bit at }a]
\le
\underbrace{[2+2(c+1)K]}_{C}R_a
+\delta+\frac{D}{2(q-1)}.
$$

Current section 5 exposes this mechanism. It is not merely asserting that some corrector exists. The arbitrary auxiliary table may cause frequent rejection; the result says that it cannot frequently return a wrong bit while seldom rejecting. Preserve the event, quantifiers and actual $R_a$. [Current equation (14); `BinaryLocalCorrection.lean`; `BinaryLocalCorrectionSoundness.lean`, including `correction_error_le` and `failure_le`; `LineTableLocalCorrection.lean`.]

### 3.5 ZERO and the common witness complete the intended inference

The ideal ZERO test checks the two residuals $M(x,0)$ and $M(x,Z_H(x))-A(x)$. If $A$ does not vanish on the grid, at least one residual is nonzero. Its degree is at most $Dh$, so a uniform point and homogeneous label detect it with probability at least $(1-Dh/q)/2$. One nonzero residual is sufficient. Independence of the two detections is unnecessary.

The actual test uses the same $x,L$ for the primary bit and both certificate calls, with independent remaining call seeds. Each correction's rejection is contained in overall ZERO rejection. This bounds rejection averaged over the targets requested by ZERO; it is not a claim that every individual target has rejection at most $\kappa$. Current line 297 now states that distinction explicitly.

Applying the corrector estimate twice gives

$$
\frac{1-Dh/q}{2}
\le (1+2C)\kappa+\delta_A+2\delta_M+\frac{D}{q-1}.
$$

For a derived cubic observation, fresh masking changes the downstream rejection probability by at most twice the full-table error. Therefore the source's transfer proof uses

$$
\frac{1-Dh/q}{2}
\le (1+2C)(\kappa+2\epsilon)+2\delta_M+\frac{D}{q-1}.
$$

The $2\epsilon$ belongs inside the existing rejection multiplier in this argument. Its placement is not cosmetic. The appropriate formal association is the derived ZERO theorem, and that association has now been added while preserving the base ZERO theorem for the preceding bound. This does not refute a different valid analysis in the source paper; it identifies what the implementation proof actually does. [Current equations (15)–(16); `BinaryZeroOnGridSoundness.lean`; `DerivedZeroOnGridSoundness.lean`, especially `rejection_transfer` and `rejectionRate_lower_bound`.]

Finally, section 6 fixes $P,Q,M_A,M_B$ once. The received-bit difference test becomes an ideal comparison after three replacements, at cost $3\epsilon$. Its nonzero residual would have support at least $1-D/q$, so sufficiently small rejection forces the polynomial identity $Q=P(X)-P(Y)$. The two derived constraints have degrees at most $3D$ and $5D$. Applying ZERO with the same certificates forces both grid-vanishing conditions, recovering a colouring. The source assembly constructs and passes exactly these common witnesses together. This is the inference the paper promised, and it is now genuinely present. [Current equations (17)–(20); `ThreeColorVerifierSoundness.lean`; `ThreeColorDecodedSoundness.lean`.]

## 5. New local findings outside the old ledger

### N01. Say what the constant-bit procedure actually returns

Current line 32 says correction recovers the line's value. That phrasing risks suggesting that the verifier reconstructs a whole field element from constantly many bits. Its operation recovers the required homogeneous binary observation of that value. The later formal description is correct, so this is an opening precision issue, not a failure of query complexity.

The proposed draft changes it to “recovers the required homogeneous binary observation of its value at the digit-substitution point.” It also preserves the distinction between local verifier operations and global decodings selected only in the soundness proof.

### N02. Make the row-to-table averaging step explicit

Current line 307 states a bound for a row with full-label error $\epsilon$; section 6 spends the global table error supplied by outer decoding. The correct bridge is one sentence. Let $\epsilon_x$ be the row error at point $x$. Conditional on $x$, fresh masking has replacement loss at most $2\epsilon_x$, regardless of the requested label. Averaging the uniform $x$ gives $2\mathbb E_x\epsilon_x\le2\epsilon$, using the full-table bound.

The source already proves this through `HadamardDerivedOracle.fullDistance` and the derived rejection-transfer theorem. The proposed paragraph supplies the missing explanation. It must not assume every row is individually $\epsilon$-close.

### N03. Give the auxiliary row's domain before asking the reader to test it

The current inventory lists the primary point domains but never states the auxiliary row's full type. For a primary point domain $F^k$, the whole auxiliary table has an oriented-line index $(u,v)\in F^k\times F^k$, and the row has type

$$
h_{u,v}:F^{cb}\times\mathcal B_1\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2.
$$

The first remaining coordinate is an inner point; the second is a label. This explains why $\Phi(z)$ is a legal requested inner point, why an affine-label row is involved, and what a homogeneous observation selects within that row. The proposed draft supplies this immediately after the early inventory. This is a more useful addition than another untyped list of operations.

ABSSW also provides a useful example of dividing proof responsibility. Its introduction routes syntactic-test proofs and correction analyses to appendices. Its local-correction section states the task before the procedure and makes clear that the spatial point is fixed while the label may remain random. It verifies the full/homogeneous counterexample above and the two-query mask bound, with a nonlinear requested label attaining $2\epsilon$ in that construction. It also distinguishes the two spatial samplers. In a 16-element field, with a fixed target $a=0$, nonzero $z$ makes $zv$ uniform. Allowing all $z$ puts probability $31/256$ on the target and $15/256$ on every other point. Restoring an independent uniform starting point $u$ makes $u+zv$ uniform even when $z=0$. That is a concrete explanation of why the outer test and fixed-target corrector legitimately use different parameter domains.

## 9. The next edition should be judged by what a reader can reconstruct

The proposed revision should be integrated as one coordinated edit. First accept or adjust its early inventory, label types and supplier boundaries; then reconcile source associations and render the resulting paper. Splitting ED02, moving ED03's inventory, and genuinely separating ED09 are parts of the same change in organization. N01 and N02 are small precision edits that should be carried with it. The minor live header collision can be repaired separately.

The following reconstruction tasks test the paper's actual purpose. They are useful for the author as well as for any later reader trial; they are not reported human-test results or a requirement to recruit an external approver.

1. **Name a physical query.** Given an auxiliary row $h_{u,v}$, identify its line index, inner point, label and stored bit. Explain why the verifier does not globally decode the polynomial.
2. **Explain both encoding costs.** Say why field symbols are not single proof bits, and why encoding the entire growing line-coefficient vector directly threatens proof length and randomness.
3. **Use the right error domain.** Explain why a full-label bound cannot be restricted to homogeneous labels, and identify the check that repairs this problem for the same witness.
4. **Recover the corrector bound.** Account for both $e$ terms, both $R_a$ terms, the primary $\delta$ and the half-root loss. Explain why shared inner randomness is allowed and why the guarantee averages the label.
5. **Distinguish the spatial samplers.** Explain why the outer test can take $z=0$, why the fixed-target analysis uses $z\ne0$, and how whole-field early acceptance later produces fair bits.
6. **Recover derived ZERO.** Start with the base bound, condition on a primary row, average its fresh-mask error and put that error inside the existing rejection multiplier.
7. **Keep one tuple.** State exactly when $P,Q,M_A,M_B$ are chosen and why the later difference and vanishing conclusions hold for those choices together.
8. **State the final resources accurately.** Distinguish the unamplified 187 bound, amplified constant queries, polynomial address bound, concrete machine runtime and the reference-NP bridge.

A successful main paper lets the reader perform those tasks while treating the named low-degree suppliers as imported theorems. If the reader still needs the long companion to discover what the corrector returns or how (16) is obtained, the main paper has not yet met its chosen purpose. If the companion is needed to prove the bivariate agreement supplier from scratch, that is the declared division of labour working as intended.

The revised section 5 is worth preserving because it already makes the hardest local inference inspectable. The next edit should make that explanation easier to find and easier to use, while keeping its exact mathematical commitments.
