# Guide to the mathematical representations

References are to the paper. A definition specifies an object or a condition; a theorem asserts an implication with its stated hypotheses. The annotations explain those hypotheses in mathematical terms.

| Object | Representation and comparison |
|---|---|
| **Velocity, pressure and space** | Space is Euclidean $\mathbb R^3$ with Lebesgue measure. The velocity $u$ and the scalar pressure $p$ are functions on $\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3$. Smoothness, the divergence condition and the equations are separate requirements. In the extended certificate a supplied vector field whose space-time integral against every divergence-free test field vanishes stands for the pressure gradient; that condition alone does not name a classical scalar pressure. |
| **Global smooth solution** | Smoothness is required on $[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R^3$, including its boundary, with the initial datum at $t=0$ and the equations at $t>0$. The functions also have values at negative times, which these hypotheses do not restrict. A two-sided time derivative at $t=0$ is not asserted. |
| **Torus** | Functions on $\mathbb R^3$ of period one in each coordinate represent the torus. The formal nonexistence conclusion requires a periodic velocity and allows every smooth pressure. The paper's convention in §1.1 and the erratum appended to [53] require a periodic pressure as well. Relating the two classes uses an additional argument: the pressure of a smooth solution with periodic velocity is periodic up to $x\cdot a(t)+C(t)$, and a change of frame removes that term. The formal proof contains this argument; the paper does not write it out. |
| **Time and weak perturbations** | Physical time $t$, self-similar time $s$ and normalized time $\theta=s/S$ are distinct. Valid certificates have $S>0$ and period one in $\theta$. Period integrals use $[0,1]\times\mathbb R^3$; endpoints have zero measure. A perturbation $v$ comes with supplied weak derivatives $G,H$ and a field $g$. At almost every $\theta$, $v(\theta)\in L^6$, $G=\nabla v$ and $H=\nabla G$ in the weak sense, and $\operatorname{div}v=0$; over a period $G$, $\operatorname{tr}H$ and $g$ are square integrable, and $v$ itself is not required to be. Tested against space-time test fields, $g=S^{-1}\partial_\theta v+\tfrac12v+\tfrac12(y\cdot\nabla)v$. The paper also chooses a representative continuous in time. |
| **Norms and indices** | Vectors have the Euclidean norm. Sums of squared matrix entries give the squared Frobenius norm; norms of derivative maps are operator norms. Changing between them can change constants. Formal derivative arrays put the output component first, which transposes the paper's convention $(\nabla v)_{jk}=\partial_jv_k$ of (7.11) and the direction-first coefficients of (B.10). The paper's label $(\iota_1,\iota_2)$ gives the time and angle frequencies $(2\iota_2-\iota_1,\varkappa\iota_1)$. |
| **Recurrent profiles** | The smooth hypothesis requires (1.2), $S>0$ and $W(s+S,y)=RW(s,R^{-1}y)$, with $R$ orthogonal and possibly orientation reversing. Pressure recurrence is not required. Hessian and pressure-gradient decay are explicit hypotheses beyond the bounds displayed in (1.3); Remark 1.2 supplies the Hessian bound and states no pressure-gradient bound. The weak conclusion has measurable representatives, explicit distributional identities, and no pressure or pointwise decay. |
| **Certificates and rows** | Data specify functions, arrays and proposed bounds; the certificate conditions require their identities and inequalities. The earlier box-and-ball estimates and the steady fractional variant are distinct from the extended classical certificate. The paper's row field equals $\sqrt{\tau}$ times the formal unit-normalized row, with the weight $\tau$ stored separately. At cell level the stored number is $c\tau$, with $c=3/50$ in the final system. |
| **Nonexistence conclusions** | For every $\nu>0$ there is a smooth divergence-free datum from which no global smooth unforced solution exists. On $\mathbb R^3$ the datum has compact support and the excluded solutions have kinetic energy bounded uniformly in $t\ge0$; on the torus the datum and the velocity of the excluded solutions are periodic in space. The datum may depend on $\nu$. The conclusions omit the finite blow-up time, the rates and the convergence to the profile of Theorem 1.1. The cell-system implications assume the full certificate conditions. |

**Conventions affecting meaning.** Natural-number subtraction stops at zero; some separate majorants use integer exponents. Division by zero, and the real square root of a negative argument, give zero. Each derivative operation gives zero where its own derivative fails; an iterated derivative differentiates the assigned first-derivative function, so there is no blanket zero rule for a Hessian. A nonintegrable ordinary integral or a nonsummable real, complex or vector series gives zero. Nonnegative integrals and series may be infinite, and conversion of infinity to a real number gives zero. A real supremum of an unbounded set gives zero. These assignments supply values outside the usual analytic hypotheses; they provide no regularity, finiteness or estimate. The glossary states the hypotheses needed for the analytic interpretation.

**Proofs.** The statement files give each theorem with its proof left open, so that an independent checker can compare them with the proofs supplied separately in the formalization. The formalization contains no certificate data, and the numerical verification of a certificate is not part of it (§1.5).
