## Harmless differences

**Corollary 8.3: the same conclusion by a different argument.** The paper obtains divergence of the vorticity integral from the periodic Beale–Kato–Majda criterion [9, 11]. The formalization proves that conclusion directly for the constructed solution. Pointwise divergence alone would not suffice. On each sufficiently late activation interval, with $\mu=\lambda_{j-1}$, it has a lower vorticity bound $c\mu^{1/8}-C\mu^{1/32}-C$, with $c>0$ and fixed constants. The crossing estimates give $r_j(t_{j+1}-t_j)\ge2L_j^2$ and $r_j\le C_r\mu^{1/8}L_j$, with $C_r>0$. Thus the integral on that interval is eventually at least a positive constant times $L_j$, which tends to infinity. Spatial continuity makes the vorticity at the origin a lower bound for the spatial essential supremum. The gradient integral follows from $\|\operatorname{curl}u\|_\infty\le C\|\nabla u\|_\infty$. These quantitative premises are proved for the selected construction. The general continuation criterion is not thereby formalized.

The formal statement separates complex Banach-valued fields, continuous real velocities and the Bernstein estimate for continuous fields in real normed spaces. Its symbol may be even rather than radial, and its scale satisfies $\Lambda>0$ rather than $\Lambda\ge1$. These enlarged ranges contain the construction's choices. Crucially, the exponent $s-M$ in (B.2) is an integer difference and retains the reciprocal-power gain when $s<M$. The convolution argument agrees with the mechanism cited in [55, Appendix A].
