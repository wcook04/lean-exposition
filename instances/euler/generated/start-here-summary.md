Alpöge, Buckmaster and Coiculescu build smooth odd periodic initial data whose Euler solution keeps bounded velocity while the vorticity at the origin becomes infinite at a finite time. A Craik–Criminale wave over a shear grows by a large factor and, at a suitable crossing time, supplies the shear that amplifies the next, higher-frequency wave. Each wave is localized near the origin with a profile that keeps its central gradient and bounds the pressure Hessian from above. Its error is removed in two parts: an endpoint problem chooses the initial value of the phase-mean correction, and a weighted transverse estimate controls the oscillation. A finite expansion, a time cutoff and a Fourier truncation leave a small residual, and one parameter schedule repeats the step indefinitely. The approximations converge to an exact Euler solution with the stated terminal behavior. Read the route to follow each step beside its formal statement.
